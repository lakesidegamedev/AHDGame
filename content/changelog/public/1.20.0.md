*Dev diary by the Lakeside Games team, 12 November 2026.*

## TL;DR

- New: a 2027 start date. The United States and Canada are open to players, the United Kingdom, Germany, Japan and China run on their own, and sixteen more countries sit in the background.
- New: Canada, with its 343-seat House of Commons, provincial legislatures, Premiers, cabinet and its own dollar, and a Quebec sovereignty referendum.
- New: countries can launch cyber operations against each other, from the intelligence console.
- New: crises open on their own when the economy stays in trouble, and they leave blame, scars and forced events behind.
- New: politics keeps moving to 2050, with party splits, ageing politicians, realigning voters and leaders whose legacy colours the next election.
- New: laws for 2027 to 2050, research tiers for 2039 and 2049, and frontier programmes.
- Changed: the economy got a long list of fixes to how plants, wages, prices and government spending behave.
- New: a landing page for the 2027 world, a Canada wiki page and a redesigned bill page.

## Which worlds get what

Many of the new systems change how a running world behaves, so they do not switch on by themselves there. Each one has a switch that staff turn on when the world is ready. Worlds created fresh from the 2027 start have them on from day one.

- **Fresh 2027 worlds only:** the country tiers, Canada as a player country, background countries, value added growth measurement, import tariffs in the producer market, computer-run party splits, politician turnover, and the 2027 starting data.
- **On in fresh worlds, off in the running world until staff switch them on:** cyber operations, GDP that grows with prices (so debt and revenue ratios stay honest over decades), AI austerity and debt restructuring, crises that open on their own, the new crisis stages, succession for empty leadership offices, leader legacies, campaign money that follows the live economy, by-elections that fill empty seats, Supreme Court nominations by computer-run Presidents, the pending docket for surprise cases, and the event fallback for players who have seen every dated event.
- **Off everywhere until staff switch them on:** the wage and price changes listed under the economy below.
- **All worlds:** everything else here, including the fixes, the redesigned bill page, the Canada and Referendums wiki pages and the interface changes. Era progression, sector technology trees, the Cold War, conflicts, automatic crises and disasters, and living conflicts are now permanent parts of the game. They were already on in every world, so play does not change.

Nothing in this list changes a running world's numbers the day the update arrives.

## The 2027 world

A 2027 world has three tiers of country.

- **Player countries:** the United States and Canada. You can create a character, run for office, found corporations and trade.
- **Computer-run countries:** the United Kingdom, Germany, Japan and China run the full simulation with computer politicians. Each can be opened to players later without a reset.
- **Background countries:** Ireland, Brazil, Nigeria, Russia, Poland, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, France, Italy, Spain, Sweden, Turkey, Austria, Finland and Greece. Each has a ruling party or coalition, a head of government who ages and retires, an approval rating tied to the economy, elections on its own schedule, a national budget that issues government bonds in its own currency, and one national index fund players can buy. They have no companies, sectors or unions of their own yet, and they do trade in the producer market, set tariffs and sanctions, pick sides, and restructure their debt when it spirals. This keeps the hourly turn fast.

Other details of the 2027 start:

- It starts from 2027 numbers: seated president, current interest rates, a realistic oldest age and dependency ratio, and US seats that follow the November 2026 elections.
- Every politician is fictional.
- Births and life expectancy follow each country's trend, and long-run growth, interest rates and score bands follow a path to 2050.
- Climate change, automation, ageing, housing and education pressures build over the years, and new technology reaches countries at different speeds.
- Exchange rates are current, with the modern Turkish lira and new currency markets, and 10 and 30 year government bonds exist.
- A limit on piled-up crises keeps the calendar readable. There are fifty-four new American events and events for the 2020s to 2050s, including a second pandemic, an AI labour shock, climate migration, the Arctic, resistant infections, orbital debris and a fusion breakthrough.
- Other start dates are unchanged.

## Canada and Quebec

- Canada opens to players in fresh 2027 worlds. Thirteen provinces and territories elect 343 MPs, and each has its own legislature and election date, 778 seats in all. Premiers' offices fill at the first provincial elections.
- Canada has its own parties, voters, characters, events, law book, tax rates, national budget, nineteen seat cabinet, Parliament Hill pages, province map, bills that move through Parliament, and a Canadian dollar on the exchange. Empty Commons seats are filled by by-elections.
- A Getting started in Canada guide and a Canada wiki page explain the country and what it loses if Quebec leaves.
- Once Quebec's Sovereignty Desire reaches 60, the Premier can request a referendum, and Canada's Prime Minister decides whether to grant it. If Yes wins and the House of Commons passes the independence bill, Quebec leaves as a new country with eight regions, a 125-seat National Assembly and the Canadian dollar. Canada's Parliament then counts only the seats that remain.
- Independence now works for any country that can split, not only the United Kingdom.

## Cyber operations

- The head of intelligence can launch a cyber operation against another country from the intelligence console. It costs money from the intelligence appropriation.
- Success depends on your cyber strength against the target's cyber resilience, a score from 0 to 1 that comes from its mandate, research spending and utilities.
- A successful operation opens a Cyber Attack crisis on the target, with grid outages and lost output. A failed one does nothing to the target.
- The target may trace the operation to its sponsor. If so, world tension rises, the news names the sponsor and the sponsor's government loses approval for a few turns.
- Either side can publicly stand down, which opens a long truce between the pair. Computer-run and background governments take part too.
- Cyber operations are on in fresh 2027 worlds and wait for a switch on the running world.

## Crises

- Recessions, banking panics, debt crises, regional job shocks, energy shortages and waves of unrest open on their own when the conditions behind them hold for several turns. The same kind cannot reopen in the same place for 24 turns.
- How bad a crisis gets follows unemployment, growth, inflation, bank stress, borrowing costs, debt and public mood. Crises cut factory output, move prices and borrowing costs, strain banks, push people out of a region and cost the government approval and votes. They also spread between countries.
- Responding to a crisis spends your actions for the turn, up to three, so it competes with campaigning and running a business. A failed response gives the actions back.
- Blame follows exposure. A governor answers for a crisis in their own state, a CEO for one in their sector and region, and party leaders and legislatures for national ones. Office holders who acted gain approval, and those who sat on their hands lose it.
- Severe crises leave scars that heal slowly, and a crisis that stays severe forces events: strikes, bank runs, a push toward default or a state of emergency.
- There is a Crises page with a world map, and you can respond and pledge aid from there. A failed aid pledge returns the money.
- Seizing a foreign-owned company now raises world tension.

## Politics over decades

- When a head of government leaves office, the game scores their time from approval, laws passed, crisis handling and scars. The legacy shows on executive and party pages and gives their party a small boost or penalty that fades.
- Empty presidencies and prime ministerships fill by clear succession rules, with caretaker governments, and the news says so. Vacant seats are filled by by-elections, and Supreme Court seats are filled by computer-run Presidents when no player holds the office.
- In countries run by computer politicians, parties can split after long or poor governments, new parties can form where the political map is empty, and parties stuck under 2% fold into their nearest neighbour. Politicians age, retire from 70 and die.
- Voters keep realigning to 2050: degree holders and others drift apart on social questions, younger voters lean toward climate action and housing, and unemployment or migration can move whole groups. Your laws can push back.
- Special presidential elections use two rounds, proportional parliaments no longer hand every seat to one party, and committee proposals show how many yes votes they need.
- Voter groups now run entirely on census groups in every election, poll and approval figure.

## Economy

The first four groups are off until staff switch them on, so they change nothing in a running world today.

- **Wages:** plants can raise wages toward a share of the value they add, wages can follow local unemployment, and the national minimum wage law can set the wage floor. Households then earn and spend more.
- **Prices:** cheaper goods can attract more buyers, prices approach their ceiling instead of stopping on it, and prices in short markets can climb again.
- **Plants:** plants selling at a loss cut back instead of running until the money runs out, plants with unsold stock make less, and plants can recover their output after a shortage.
- **Government buying:** budget lines draw down saved money gradually, so sellers no longer see one huge burst of revenue.

These apply to all worlds, and the budget now uses one GDP figure throughout.

- Plants and supply: sector pages explain why output did not sell and what would run more, own-site supply can be priced at cost or at market, and you can have a sector sell to outside buyers before your own plants.
- Sites with construction loans can change hands, shareholders can see private corporations they own, and net worth counts shares your companies hold in other companies.
- Government fines, fees and privatization proceeds reach spendable Treasury cash. Income tax tiers keep up with prices, and unpaid income tax is collected from your other currency wallets.
- Fresh 2027 worlds measure growth by value added, and import tariffs apply to every cross-border sale in the producer market.
- Research breakthroughs no longer inflate mineral deposits without limit.
- Banks reopen in former Eastern Bloc countries once they have market economies.

## Research

- A 2039 tier adds new production methods in twelve sectors. A 2049 tier gives every sector a full lane, with breakthroughs such as Commercial Fusion, Space Resources and Gene Therapy at Scale.
- Frontier programme races for the 2040s offer real output, and unpaid points cannot win them.
- Every new method lists its build time and its downside. Automated methods cut crews only once they are built, and old methods can retire.

## Laws

- Twelve new laws arrive in 2027 and 2030, such as AI liability, data protection, housing supply, a critical minerals stockpile, a cyber resilience mandate, an automation tax, wage insurance and a carbon border adjustment. More arrive for 2035, 2040, 2045 and 2050, and some old laws retire.
- The 2030s add a real fight over who pays for automation, with retraining, a hiring subsidy, an automation pension levy and transition compacts.
- Germany, Japan and China now each run on their own law book, and the US and UK law books were rewritten as plain data.
- Passed Japanese laws and regional laws now move regional political lean, as US laws already did.
- New laws start with no statute in force, so nothing changes in a country until a bill passes.

## The interface and the wiki

- The 2027 world has its own landing page, with a news ticker along the globe from 2027 to 2050 and a country list that shows the real tiers.
- Bill pages were redesigned. A new header shows the stage, time left and counts for the voting chamber, and the seating chart sits beside the vote tally.
- The presidential map opens into Campaign mode: click a state to act there, see field offices down to counties and open or close one without leaving the map. Campaign managers can build Campaign Presence.
- Ask Keir offers Concise or Standard answers and recovers when the connection drops mid-answer. Bell notifications open the item you clicked, and Mark all read works on long lists.
- Singleplayer says plainly when an era cannot be started, and the desktop download builds again for Apple Silicon Macs. Game links can open the iPhone and iPad app.
- Pages for regions, corporations and campaigns load lighter, and the hourly turn does less work.

## Fixes

- A cyber operation sent twice at once is charged once, and only an attacker can stand down.
- Crisis decisions can no longer be paid or applied twice, and failed aid is refunded.
- Seats no longer vanish when a party bloc's holder leaves, and a banned party officer no longer blocks a seat.
- Alt checks only count accounts that share one device fingerprint, so fewer players are flagged by mistake.
- Opening an inbox item no longer jumps to another one.
- Index funds no longer get stuck on holders that no longer exist.
- Turns no longer skip the economy after a restart in the middle of one.
- A new world starts clean, and a reset refuses to run if it would leave the old law book behind.
