## What landed in this release

118 changes shipped.

### Elections and campaigns

- **A market adjustment now costs the government approval, by what actually happened**
  While a Market Adjustment is open, the government's approval carries a named "Market adjustment" cost measured from how much of the country's capacity is losing money and, later, how far unemployment has risen. Pressing a response button earns nothing by itself. A wire headline marks several countries adjusting at once. Switched on separately.
- **Connect metric funding, outcomes and national readouts**
  Metric estimates share current statutory funding evidence and feed compatible gameplay consumers. Trade and turnout use their actual ledgers, and national rates use owner counts where available.
- **House of Councillors Election Night shows the whole chamber**
  Election Night for the House of Councillors now counts the seats not up for election, so the national picture shows the whole chamber and the right majority.
- **House of Councillors projection keeps seats not up for election**
  The elections page no longer shows the half of the House of Councillors that is not contested as empty.
- **Founding presidential terms do not count toward the limit**
  A presidential term won in the founding election no longer counts toward the two-term limit.

### Government and legislation

- **Big CEO pay rises need a shareholder vote**
  A player CEO who raises their own daily salary above a set share of the company's recent net income now has to put it to the shareholders. Lowering pay and small rises stay free. Switched on separately.
- **Competition authorities can now break up dominant groups**
  Where the Competition Act is at structural enforcement or higher, a dominant group can be ordered to sell down.
- **Every country can now pass competition, subsidiary and foreign investment laws**
  Three new laws let legislatures police mergers, cap corporate groups and limit foreign-controlled companies.
- **Foreign takeovers are now screened where the law says so**
  Under Investment Screening or a share cap, a takeover by a group controlled from abroad goes to the competition authority first.
- **Subsidiary and foreign investment laws now bind**
  Groups over a country's limits must give up control within 48 turns, or their stake is sold at book value.
- **Connect poverty outcomes to funded laws**
  Poverty estimates respond to existing enacted laws and their actual funded delivery across supported countries. Old adjustments fade gradually, and the boards identify poverty as a modeled population-share estimate.
- **Browse bill proposals during active bills**
  Eligible Japanese cabinet members can open and prepare a bill while another bill is in progress. Submission stays disabled until the existing bill resolves.
- **The hourly turn reads a third less and stops retrying dead settlements**
  Settlement recovery no longer walks payouts whose recipient was deleted, the sovereign coupon queue is bounded by the cash a Treasury actually holds, and the turn's heaviest collection reads now project only the fields they use. The benchmark turn returns 27.5% less BSON and a fifth fewer settlement reads.
- **Nomination voting deadlines**
  Cabinet nomination countdowns now follow their actual closing turn.
- **Regional economic bill proposals**

### War and diplomacy

- **Market Adjustment, a measured downturn event for the funded market**
  When a large share of a country's capacity is losing money and output is shrinking, a Market Adjustment event now opens. It adds no damage of its own; it names the situation and puts a real treasury response in front of the government. Switched on separately.

## Detailed release notes

### Corporate strength has to be maintained

#### What changed

- Marketing strength loses 1.5% a turn, so it reflects recent marketing spend rather than a lifetime total.
- Group synergy still pulls members toward the group's strongest company, but the strongest company now pays what the members gain.
- Split rival plants reads each company's own marketing strength for both the share and the odds.
- A tech dominance shield removes at most half of the dominance toll, and only techs from the current and previous decade count. The Tech tab marks older shield techs as expired.
- Each part is switched on separately, one turn apart, and can be switched back.

### Management overhead counts the whole corporate group

#### What changed

- Plant count for management overhead is the total across a corporation's control group: the top corporation and every corporation it controls through more than half of the votes, directly or through other subsidiaries.
- Every member pays the group's overhead on its own sectors. A subsidiary is charged the same rate as its parent.
- State-owned corporations stay exempt.
- The sector page shows the overhead the turn actually applied.

### Market switch preparation

- Under the upcoming market, a plant pays wages and running costs for the share of its line it actually runs. A plant cut back to 20% pays 20% of its wages, and its idle workers become unemployed.
- Under the upcoming market, plants run less when each unit sold no longer covers its inputs, wages and running costs.

### Market switch: transition options

- When the new market goes live, a business whose real demand is far below what it used to earn shows a Market transition panel for a limited number of turns.
- During that window it gets fading revenue support, and its owner can convert it once to another industry for free, keeping its value, or mothball it with no upkeep.
- A first retool inside the same industry is also free during the window.
- AI companies use the same options when another industry is clearly short.
- The build planner and state economy pages now show real unmet demand, after plants already under construction, instead of unclaimed market share.

### Pass-through companies are taxed like the real thing

- Owners of an LLC, S-Corp or LLP are now taxed on their full share of the company's profit every turn, whether it is paid out or not. The company pays this for them at the headquarters country's and state's rates, so payouts are not taxed a second time.
- A pass-through whose shares trade publicly is taxed as an ordinary company, as is an S-Corp with more than 100 holders or any holder that is not a person.
- A Godo Kaisha (GK) is now taxed as an ordinary company, as in Japan.
- The change phases in over 24 turns. Your company page shows how your legal form is taxed, and the financials show the owner tax on its own line.

### A market adjustment now costs the government approval, by what actually happened

#### What changed

- A visible "Market adjustment" chip joins the approval breakdown while the event is open. It is never hidden inside other numbers.
- Loss term: 4 points per 100 percent of capacity losing money above 40 percent, capped at 2.
- Unemployment term: half a point per percentage point of unemployment above its level when the adjustment began, capped at 2. It stays off until unemployment is confirmed to follow the funded economy.
- The chip is capped at 4 points per country and fades over 12 turns after the event ends.
- Responses count only through the losses and jobs they actually change.
- When three or more countries are adjusting at once the news wire carries a world headline, and another when it eases below two. Headlines carry no effects.

### Big CEO pay rises need a shareholder vote

#### What changed

- Saving a salary above the allowed share of trailing daily net income opens a CEO pay vote instead of changing the salary. The rest of the save still applies, and the salary stays the same until the vote passes.
- If the company has no positive net income, any pay rise needs a vote.
- Lowering pay, holding it, and rises within the share need no vote.
- Private companies, NPP-run companies and state-owned companies are not affected.

### Closing funds pay out queued withdrawals

#### What changed

- A winding-down fund with only cash left now pays its queued withdrawals, priced on the cash it actually holds, so queued holders and remaining holders get the same price per unit.
- Once the queue is empty the fund closes and pays its remaining holders as before.
- Funds still selling shares or bonds keep their queue until those sales finish.

### Competition authorities can now break up dominant groups

- In a country whose Competition Act is at Structural Enforcement or Open Markets Charter, a group that holds more of a national industry than the act allows (half, or 40% at the top level) gets a competition case.
- The minister who reviews mergers has 6 turns to order a breakup or dismiss the case, from the company's page. If they do not decide, the case follows the measured share: still above the limit means a breakup order.
- A breakup order gives the group 12 turns to bring its share below the limit. Until then it cannot make new acquisitions, and once late it is fined each turn, the same as a merger remedy.

### Construction loans and overdrawn cash now count against a company

#### What changed

- Outstanding bank construction loan principal counts as debt in a company's credit score and in its share price, wherever the plant under construction counts as an asset.
- Overdrawn cash counts as the negative it is in the share price instead of being treated as zero.
- Companies that paid for their builds in cash are not affected.

### Every country can now pass competition, subsidiary and foreign investment laws

- Every country with a legislature can now pass a Competition Act. It sets when a merger or takeover goes to the competition authority, and at higher levels lets the authority order a dominant group to sell parts of its business. Most countries start with merger review for deals above 60% of a national industry.
- A new Subsidiary Limits Act caps how many subsidiaries one corporate group may run in the country, from no limit down to three.
- A new Foreign Investment Act sets how far groups controlled from abroad may operate, from fully open, through screening and share caps, to a ban.
- Both new acts start open. Enforcement of the subsidiary and foreign investment limits arrives in a follow-up update; until then these laws can be debated and passed but do not yet bind.

### Foreign takeovers are now screened where the law says so

- Where a country's Foreign Investment Act is at Investment Screening or one of the share caps, an acquisition or takeover by a group headquartered abroad is referred to the minister who reviews mergers, even when it is too small for a competition review.
- The minister has 6 turns to block it. If they do nothing, the deal is cleared.
- In those countries a foreign-controlled group cannot buy control of a company on the stock market; it has to make an acquisition offer so the deal can be screened.

### Holding a market now has a running cost that fades as your share falls

#### What changed

- A group's share of a market is the sum of its members' shares there. Above half of a local market, or 30 percent of a nation's, the group pays a margin penalty that grows with share.
- The cost falls away smoothly as share falls back to the threshold. A group at the threshold pays nothing.
- Your tech shield still reduces it. State-owned corporations stay exempt.
- The sector page shows the penalty the turn actually applied.

### Jobs follow what funded plants actually run

#### What changed

- A funded plant's workers and wanted workers are the staffed headcount its production plan actually uses, not a figure implied by its full capacity.
- The labour demand recorded for each region adds up those funded headcounts, so unemployment, approval and elections read how many people the funded economy really employs.
- A plant that does not run employs no one.
- Nothing changes until the funded market is live and this is switched on.

### Long supply contracts take longer notice to cancel

- The masthead says Majority owner in the same case instead of Subsidiary of.
- A formalized subsidiary still shows Parent on the Structure panel and Subsidiary of on the masthead.

### A winding-down fund now finishes instead of stalling on leftover orders

#### What changed

- Wind-up cancels the fund's open share orders and returns their unspent escrow to the fund.
- Queued subscriptions are refunded to the investor through the same path a player cancel uses.
- Only then are queued withdrawals paid and the fund delisted, so the turn warning stops instead of repeating.

### Broken manifesto pledges now bleed UK government confidence each turn

#### What changed

- Each turn judges the governing party's most recent locked manifesto against the national laws in force and feeds the kept share into the confidence gauge.
- A government with no formed administration, no locked manifesto, or no recognised pledges gets no pledge bleed at all, so an unjudgeable government is not treated as having broken everything.
- The confidence gauge's existing broken-promise bleed is unchanged; it is now actually supplied.

### Clearer share holding totals when selling

Share sell forms now distinguish your total ownership from the shares available in the selected short-term or long-term holding. Remaining-share labels also identify the holding being sold.

### Continue with Discord now sends the redirect host the app registers

#### What changed

- The Discord authorize request and the token exchange both read one helper, so they can never send different redirect addresses.
- A www host is rewritten to the apex, which is the address the Discord application registers.
- Local development and apex deployments are unchanged.

### Cost-plus quotes no longer spiral when a plant runs a fraction of its capacity

#### What changed

- A cost-plus plant's fixed cost per unit is capped at what the demand throttle's 10 percent floor would charge.
- Input cost still scales with the recipe, so only the fixed share was bounded.
- Plants running at or above the floor record the same basis as before.

### Distinguish sector run limits

#### What changed

- Uses a distinct color for worker shortages so they are easier to tell apart from input shortages.

### Every reason a plant is idle now has its own colour

#### What changed

- Each idle cause draws with its own colour on the run meter instead of borrowing its severity tone.
- The legend dot for a cause is drawn from the same colour as its bar segment, so the two always match.
- The test suite fails if two causes are ever given the same colour again.

### Explain ongoing costs before a sector split

The sector split planner now distinguishes the upfront attack charge from ongoing labour, input and upkeep costs, and explains that acquiring more plants does not guarantee demand or profit.

### Fair research breakthrough targets

- Research no longer repeatedly picks the first operation when several operations have the same capacity priority.
- Research now correctly prioritizes operations that can produce the smallest fraction of their desired output. Breakthrough odds and improvement size are unchanged.

### Improve slow action diagnostics

#### What changed

- Added privacy-safe timing summaries for campaign action and Political Strength budget requests.

### More stable export pricing

Successful exports no longer push a fully sold national offer above an eligible competitor with enough unsold supply to replace it. Price feedback accounts for freight and tariffs while buyers continue choosing the cheapest landed supply first.

### Nomination voting deadlines

- Active cabinet nominations show remaining turns instead of a misleading expired clock date after delayed or paused turns.
- Once voting reaches its closing turn, the page shows that the result is pending.

### Obsolete sector strategies fall back to the standard recipe, not a locked future one

#### What changed

- An unknown or retired strategy ID resolves to the sector's standard recipe instead of the first catalog entry.
- Valid explicit strategies, including unlocked advanced ones, resolve exactly as before.
- Unknown sector types stay inert and still report once.
- No persisted plant capacity, output units or transition state is rewritten; this is a resolution fix only.

### Private companies no longer take order book trades

#### What changed

- Placing a limit order on a private company is refused with a message pointing to private sale.
- The trade dialog drops Limit Order and Orderbook for private companies, leaving At Market and Private Deal.
- Existing open orders stay visible to every holder, so nobody is left with a resting bid only its owner can see.

### Regional economic bill proposals

Regional economic bills no longer fail category validation when drafted under the Economy, Work, Trade and Financial Affairs domain.

### Spin-offs now warn about the seven day cooldown and count it down

#### What changed

- The spin-off dialog warns that a spin-off starts a 168 turn cooldown, about a week, before you commit.
- While the cooldown runs, the dialog shows how many turns are left and disables the spin-off button instead of letting the request fail.
- The cooldown itself is unchanged at 168 turns, and the server and the page count it down from the same helper.

### The producer market shadow totals spend per fill

#### What changed

- The shadow's household money check and its spend summary read buyer and
  order spend straight from the fills. A fill's supplier legs always add up to
  the fill's units, so the totals are the same.
- The expanded per-supplier trade list is still available for audits and
  route or contract accounting.

No balance, price, rate or live market input changed. The shadow is telemetry
and the live economy does not read it.

### The share price chart now colours its own span and says how long it is

#### What changed

- The masthead price line is coloured by the change across the turns it actually draws, not by the previous turn alone.
- A flat line is drawn neutral instead of green.
- The span, its change, and the low and high are labelled on phones as well as large screens.

### The turn processor no longer waits on the disk after every write

- The background turn processor can now acknowledge database writes as soon as they are applied rather than after each disk sync. On the production disk each sync took about 11 ms and a turn makes tens of thousands of writes in sequence.
- The website is unaffected and keeps confirming every write on disk.

### Turns process faster

- The turn processor now fetches the data it needs in a few larger reads instead of many small ones, and writes related records together, so a turn spends less time waiting on the database.
- Nothing about the game rules changed: the same turn still produces the same world, down to every balance.

### Waiting on inputs now has its own colour on the sector capacity chart

#### What changed

- Waiting on inputs uses its own amber swatch on the run meter fill and the legend dot.
- Demand limited keeps the warning yellow, so the two causes no longer read as one.
- Both keep warning severity, so the chart still says output is being held back rather than lost.
