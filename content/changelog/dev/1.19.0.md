1.19 is the release where the economy moved onto the funded producer market.
The world's companies and markets were rebuilt from the era seed with an
economy-only reset, and the turn then switched to a market where households, governments and companies pay real
money for every unit they buy. Most of the economy notes below are the work
that made that switch hold up: plants that keep running on credit or on their
own sales, prices that follow real shortages and what buyers actually paid,
your own sites served first, banks and bond markets that no longer create or
strand money, and an admin path to rebuild and pause the economy safely. Notes
written before the switch still say "the upcoming market". Whole plants and the
share value rules shipped switched off and are now on in the live world.

The rest of the release is about getting around the game. Campaign Manager
puts every race below the presidency on one page, Ask Keir leaves experimental
features and works on phones, and experimental players get a side tray of
widgets, grouped desktop menus and a phone dock. The wiki was rewritten system
by system and checked against the code, singleplayer gained save slots,
autosaves and multi-turn play, and both page loads and the hourly turn carry
far less data than they did in 1.18.

## What landed in this release

275 changes shipped. Eleven entries, each placed last in its section, were added in the release audit for pull requests that merged without a note.

### Market switch and the funded market

- **Market switch preparation**
  Groundwork for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change yet.
- **Closed factories reopen when their goods run short**
  Groundwork for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change yet.
- **In-house suppliers no longer look idle**
  Groundwork for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change yet.
- **Market prices follow the orders buyers actually placed**
  Groundwork for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change yet.
- **Short goods ramp back up faster**
  Groundwork for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change yet.
- **Unspent public purchasing reaches households**
  Groundwork for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change yet.
- **Market opening accepts brand-new companies**
  Internal fix for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change.
- **Market shadow records the running build**
  Internal record keeping for the upcoming market change. No gameplay change.
- **Economy reset switches straight onto the funded market**
  The economy-only reset can now rebuild the economy and move it onto the funded producer market in one operator command, with an optional economy pause horizon.
- **Economy-only reset starts the funded market with faster builds and a gentler price pace**
  The economy-only reset now writes the funded-market settings chosen on the fresh-economy tests: short plants build in a quarter of the time, and prices move at most 2% a turn up to 3 times base. Admin tooling only.
- **Economy-only reset starts the rebuilt economy from fresh-world settings**
  The economy-only reset now also clears every market lever that was staged onto the old economy, reseeds commodity prices and exchange rates at their era values, and removes the old price, trade and wealth history. Politics, budgets and debt are untouched. Admin tooling only.
- **An economy reset clears the rest of the old economy**
  An economy reset no longer carries the old economy's growth trend, executive records, union pensions, mine depletion or savings totals into the rebuilt world, and the rebuilt plants start with the market governor ramp finished.
- **An economy reset no longer leaves workers locked out of jobs**
  Each region's share of jobs held for companies is measured again from the rebuilt companies after an economy reset.
- **Economy resets respect the Soviet breakup**
  An economy reset now rebuilds the world as it is, not as the 1991 start had it. After a federation breaks up, its continuing state gets only its sovereign issuer back, never the old command-economy conglomerate.
- **Admin tool to rebuild the economy without resetting the world**
  Operators can now rebuild companies and markets from the era seed while keeping politics, budgets and the calendar. No gameplay change.
- **Economy rebuild tool handles full-size worlds**
  The admin tool that rebuilds companies and markets now stores its reviewed plan in parts, so it works on worlds of any size. No gameplay change.
- **Timed economy-only pause**
  Operators can freeze the economy for a set number of turns while elections, campaigns, parties and legislation keep running. The economy resumes on its own, and the status bar shows when.
- **Economy pause no longer stops the turn at the treasury cash step**
  During a timed economy pause the treasury cash step now skips with the bond step it depends on, so the rest of the turn (politics, elections, legislation) runs.
- **Choose your starting wealth for the new economy**
  When the fresh economy starts, every politician gets one chance to pick the wealth background they begin it with.
- **Funded market builds faster where it is short, and its price pace can be set**
  On funded turns, outstanding construction in plants whose output is short on the funded book can be shortened by a settable factor, and the funded price rule's per-turn move and ceiling can be set. All three settings are absent by default, so nothing changes until a world turns them on.
- **Funded market plants keep running until the market answers, and stock cost stays sane**
  Two rules for the funded producer market found on the fresh-economy proof. A plant that has never produced or offered anything keeps a full run instead of reading its own empty first turn as no demand. Stock carried into the next turn is valued at no more than three times the reference price, so a run that made almost nothing cannot book a billion-anchor write-off on a few units.
- **Funded market plants that sell out grow instead of shrinking on their crew bill**
  The funded market's output rules no longer cut a plant that sold out at a price above its materials cost. Its loss came from the minimum crew and site bill spread over a small run, and cutting made the per-unit bill worse every turn. Plants that never turn a profit are still mothballed by the chronic-loss rule.
- **Funded market prices follow real shortages and gluts**
  On a funded producer turn the commodity price engine now moves each scarcity multiplier by the funded market's own measured imbalance, up to 6% a turn, instead of the formula-era ratchets that were built to stop a demand treadmill. A real shortage now raises the price every turn it lasts. No change on legacy turns.
- **The funded market transition package can be switched off**
  A new configuration switch turns off the transition package of the funded producer market: no over-built classification at the opening, no struggling marks, no support payments and no free conversion window. The economy-only reset switches it off, because an economy rebuilt from the seed has nothing to land softly. Existing worlds are unchanged. Admin tooling only.
- **Fully idle plant types can restart, and AI companies read current demand**
  On the funded market, a plant type with every plant mothballed can now reopen when buyers go short, and AI companies judge market saturation from this turn's sales instead of a stale reading.
- **Funded turns finish when a company changes hands mid-turn**
  A spin-off, sale or takeover during a funded producer turn no longer stops the turn. The company that owned the plant when the turn was planned is paid for that turn; the new owner takes over next turn.
- **Producer market resumes from its recorded opening**
  A world that switched to the funded producer market late now resumes from the opening it recorded, instead of refusing every plant because it expected a more recent one.
- **The hourly turn keeps running after construction moved to the country market**
  Fixes two problems that would have stopped the first turn after construction services started trading across the whole country.
- **Company values no longer jump at a fixed turn**
  A scheduled lift to company revenue and valuations, written for the previous world, is removed so it cannot fire partway through the new economy.
- **Automatic prices stay at the market, and the plant page names the real limit**
  A plant on automatic pricing no longer asks far above the market in a short market and sells nothing. Your pricing posture now sets its markup over the market price. The capacity meter tells short inputs, short cash and weak demand apart.
- **Automatic pricing now follows what buyers actually paid**
  Plants on automatic pricing now ask around last turn's market price for their good instead of a reference that could sit well above it, so they stop pricing themselves out of the market.
- **Share prices follow what a company actually sells**
  On worlds with the funded market, a company's plants are now valued on the money they actually earn, or what was paid to build them, not on what they could make at today's prices.
- **Share prices include sentiment from the turn**
  Share prices at the turn now include investor sentiment and order flow instead of only being added fifteen minutes later, so the market chart no longer drops at each turn and recovers.
- **Share prices stop drifting down for owner-controlled public companies**
  A public company whose chief executive owned almost all of its shares could see its share price slip a few percent every turn and then jump many times over in a single turn. Prices now move at most 35% a turn and settle at the company's value.
- **Share sales and credit lines follow real company value**
  When this rule is switched on, the stock market pool stops paying more for new shares than a company owns or earns, and your credit line counts shares in your own company at a safer value. Trades between players are not affected.
- **Targeted embargoes now stop sales**
  An embargo on one commodity, or on one direction of trade, used to change nothing about who could buy from whom. Now buyers can no longer buy the embargoed goods across a route the embargo closes.
- **Freight shortages fill faster**
  States short of freight now get new trucking capacity sooner. Building logistics where it is needed no longer costs a fortune because of fleets in other countries, AI companies see each state's own shortage, and new fleet arrives in 15 turns instead of 36.
- **Freight sells to its own state before anyone else**
  A state's haulage capacity now goes to buyers in that state first, before buyers elsewhere take it from the national market. No change to prices or to any other commodity.
- **Plants no longer stall over a trace of construction work**
  Construction services now trade across your whole country, and a plant that is short only of a small input runs anyway and pays extra for the shortfall, instead of idling most of its capacity.
- **Clearer commodity markets, repeated party actions and sovereign bond risk**
- **Filter and sort supply offers**
  Supply offer lists can now be sorted by amount, price, date or commodity name in either direction, and remember your choice.
- **Supply offers now say which side you are on**
  Taking a company's offer to sell makes you the buyer, and the supply offers list now says so, so an offer to sell is no longer read as a chance to sell.
- **Supply agreements can no longer be signed for goods that can never arrive**
  A supply agreement now needs a buyer plant that actually uses the goods, and local services such as real estate need one in the contract's state. Live agreements that deliver nothing now say why.
- **AI suppliers now answer every supply proposal**
  A supply proposal to an AI-run company now gets a reply within a turn: a counter-offer if your price or volume is too much, or a decline with the reason.
- **Internal discount hidden where it had no effect**
  On worlds running the funded market, the internal supply panel no longer offers a discount that never applied.
- **Groundwork for automatic asks that rise when a plant sells out**
  A rule that raises an automatic ask one step when a plant sold everything it offered, holds it when it nearly sold out, and gives the raise back when sales fall short. Not wired into the turn yet, so nothing changes for players.
- **Unions are reset with the economy, and union heads can always resign**
  An economy-only reset now wipes and reseeds unions, organizers, leader votes and endorsements with the rebuilt companies instead of keeping unions that represented shops that no longer exist. The union page reads leadership from the union itself, so a real union head can always reach Resign leadership.

### Corporations and plants

- **Plants are built, owned and worn out whole**
  A plant is now the unit you build and own. Sectors hold whole plants, builds arrive one whole plant at a time, and wear takes a plant off only when it has worn out a whole plant. Ships switched off and turns on between turns.
- **Build orders show their real number of plants**
  Vehicle factories and entertainment media sectors now count each build order at their own plant size.
- **Builds that arrive in one piece no longer look stuck**
  An order for less than one whole plant, or for fewer plants than it has turns to build, showed a small per-turn rate such as "about 0.1 a turn" even though nothing arrives until each plant is finished. The build card and the sector page now show the turn the next plant lands, or the turn it is ready.
- **Company screens count plants, not capacity units**
  Every company table now leads with the plants you own and the plants being built, and shows one clear reading of how they did: how much of what they could make they ran, and how much of what they made sold.
- **Sector table counts plants and shows builds arriving**
  The corporation sector table now shows how many plants you own and have on order, how fast builds arrive, and how much of your capacity you really use.
- **Vehicle plants count as vehicle plants**
  A manufacturing company that switched to building cars or trucks now owns, orders and sees the right number of plants. The sector page talks about plants, not capacity.
- **Build capacity across many sectors at once**
  CEOs can now order new capacity in every sector of a country or industry from one dialog in the CEO Office.
- **Build preview shows the right crew for a sector's production method**
  The staff figure in the expand sector preview now scales with the production method the sector actually runs, instead of always using an ordinary factory's crew.
- **Building and plant changes wait for the turn to finish**
  Building, expanding, converting or abandoning a plant is paused for the minute or so a turn takes to process, so a change cannot stop the turn.
- **Expansion screen shows which markets you can enter now**
  The new-sector picker now splits markets into "Can enter now" and "Not available now" with the reason, and shows room to build with the same figure as the state market page instead of old unclaimed revenue.
- **Keep the selected market when reviewing a sector**
  Market selection no longer jumps back to the first state when a player quickly reviews a newly loaded market.
- **Your own sites get your own output first**
  When one of your plants makes something your other sites use, those sites now get it before any outside buyer, closest site first. Before, an outside buyer could sometimes take it all while your own site went short.
- **Output sent to your own sites no longer looks unsold**
  A plant that supplies your company's other sites now says so on the sector row and the sector page, instead of showing 0% sold and no revenue as if it were broken.
- **Output sent to your own sites no longer shows as unsold**
  A plant whose output went free to your company's other sites showed those units as unsold, with a warning that the market was oversupplied. The sector page now names them as delivered to your own sites.
- **Companies short on cash run smaller, not stuck**
  A company that cannot afford its whole operation now runs the share it can staff and supply, and prices respect their per-turn limits.
- **Companies short of cash can keep their plants running on credit**
  A private company that runs out of cash no longer has to idle every plant. It can keep staffing them and owe its workers and site bills for a while, paying them back first out of what it sells next. The credit is limited, so a company that keeps losing money still has to stop.
- **Plants that sell well keep running when the company is short of cash**
  A company that spent its cash on construction no longer has to idle plants whose sales would pay for their own run. Wages, site costs and materials can be paid out of that turn's sales, a plant that cannot run no longer bills a full crew, and a plant stopped for lack of cash plans its next run from real demand instead of losing its customers.
- **Plants pay wages and site costs only for what they actually make**
  A plant whose inputs ran short no longer pays a full crew and full site bill for output it never made. It pays for the share it produced, plus the small upkeep on idle capacity.
- **Mines pay materials only for what they can extract**
  Extraction sites in states that lack part of their resource mix now pay materials only for the share they can actually produce.
- **Plants that make two products follow the one that sells**
  A plant that makes more than one product no longer shuts down because one of them is piling up unsold. If another product sells out and pays the plant's running costs, the plant keeps running for it.
- **Plants with two products weigh only the costs that grow with output**
  When a plant decides whether to run harder for the product that sells out, it no longer counts the upkeep it pays on idle equipment anyway.
- **Plants that sell out ramp up again after a retool or a supply shortage**
  A plant is now judged on the products it currently makes, and a plant that sells everything it makes no longer shrinks just because its inputs ran short.
- **Small plants that sell out recover their planned output**
- **National corporations run their plants again**
  State-owned national corporations had stopped producing because they hold almost no cash of their own. Their government now funds each turn's production up front, so their plants, workers and wages are back.
- **Companies hire much faster**
  Open jobs at companies now fill in a turn or two where people are looking for work, and paying above the local going rate hires faster still.
- **See the state's worker pool beside your unfilled jobs**
  When a sector cannot fill its jobs, the workforce view now shows how many workers the state has and how many every employer there wants, so you can see the pool your shortfall comes from.
- **AI companies stop mistaking input shortages for weak demand**
  An AI-run plant that sells everything it makes, but runs below capacity because it is missing inputs, no longer counts as a crowded market. It can grow again, and new AI companies can enter markets like that.
- **AI companies and autopilot skip plants they cannot supply**
  NPP companies and corporations on autopilot no longer open or expand a plant whose key inputs its state cannot deliver.
- **New AI companies build what is actually short**
  When an AI company founds a new plant, it now picks the product line that makes the goods the market is missing. A new chemical plant can open as a fertilizer or pharmaceutical plant instead of adding more of a glutted good.
- **Media outlets with an editorial stance can grow into short markets**
  An outlet whose editorial stance cost it audience used to be held to a 15% a turn crawl even when its advertising market had buyers left over, because the audience cost was read as unsold stock. It now steps up like a neutral outlet.
- **Market leaders no longer pay running dominance costs on what they sell**
  Companies that dominate a market were charged a size cost and a compliance cost every turn, the compliance cost measured on a revenue figure the plant never earned. Under plants, dominance is paid when you build, so those running charges are gone, and old stock written down is no longer charged twice.
- **Parent companies can retool and rebrand their AI-run subsidiaries**
  If your company controls a subsidiary that an AI manager runs, you can now change that subsidiary's sector strategies and its logo yourself, and the AI manager will not undo your strategy choice for 48 turns.
- **Negotiated private share sales at any agreed price**
  Sell shares to one named player at the price you agreed, outside the usual 50% to 200% range. The buyer pays once and gets every share straight away, and the deal is shown publicly on the company's Shares tab.
- **Corporate ownership and new-player safeguards**
- **The spin-off action shows how long its cooldown has left**
  A corporation's structure page now says how many turns remain before it can spin off another subsidiary, instead of only saying so once you open the dialog.
- **Headquarters moves no longer wait on a construction loan**
  A company can now move its headquarters within the same country, or to a country that uses the same currency, while a construction loan on one of its sites is still being repaid. The loan keeps servicing from company cash as before.
- **Demand limited explains local-only services**
  When a sector making construction, freight, healthcare or real estate services is demand limited, the explanation now says those sell only in the sector's own state.
- **Sector page shows unsold output and real losses**
  The pricing panel now reports what actually sold last turn, and a plant whose output went unsold shows its real loss instead of a few dollars.
- **Sold share labels state their denominator**
  The sector pricing badge and the corporation Operations summary both showed a sold percentage that counted different things. Each now says whether it means the share of units offered or the share of output.
- **Freight details on a sector are easier to read**
  The State freight line under your plants on a sector's overview now uses normal body text instead of the smallest size.
- **Union-busting costs now show in company finance history**
  A union-busting attempt takes cash from the company, but the payment never appeared in its finance history, so large busts looked like missing money. Each attempt now records its own line.
- **Show corporate tax reform phase-in progress**
  Affected corporations now show their tax reform progress and full-taxation turn on the Finance page.
- **Fixed a rare dividend miscalculation**
  A rare share-count mismatch could make a company's dividend pay out far more than its dividend pool. Dividends now never exceed the pool.
- **Settle income taxes before payouts**
  Salary, dividends and interest settle current taxes and eligible outstanding tax debts before the remaining money becomes spendable. Corporate receipts also settle taxes and payables before optional spending.
- **Bond coupon tax shown under federal tax**
  The Financials tab now shows how much of a corporation's federal tax is charged on coupons from bonds it holds, so the tax line no longer looks too high against operating profit.
- **Bond interest a company pays is deducted before tax**
  Companies are now taxed on profit after paying their bondholders. Interest earned on bonds a company holds is still taxed, and national enterprises, which pay no bond interest, are unchanged.
- **Bond limits no longer overstate revenue earned abroad**
  The single-sale bond cap now converts each sector's revenue from the country it operates in, so corporations with overseas sectors see the right figure instead of one inflated by treating foreign money as their own.
- **Loans and buyouts count money the way it really is**
  Credit checks no longer count income from before an economy reset or treat borrowed bank money as wealth, and a company can no longer be bought for less than the cash it holds.
- **Whole plants conversion tool**
  An operator tool with a dry run recounts every sector to whole plants before the whole plants rule is switched on. The leftover part of a plant is paid back to the owner at book cost, a sector below one plant rounds up to one, builds under way are recounted the same way, and everything touched is snapshotted for rollback.
- **Build toll prices a retooling sector on its target strategy**
  The commodity dominance toll on a build no longer reads the trailing mix of a sector that is changing strategy. A mine halfway through a retool to iron was quoted about forty times the normal price because a trace of timber in its old mix looked dominant; the toll now uses the strategy the new capacity will actually run.
- **Sector Inputs panel quotes the real input shortfall**
  The callout under Things you buy now quotes the share of capacity the input shortage held idle, the same figure as the run meter, instead of the lagged availability throttle that could say a sector ran at 96% when it ran at a quarter.
- **Share sales withhold tax up front and tax arrears are collected in part**
  Personal share sales are now paid net of the tax on the realized profit, with short-term profit taxed as income and long-term profit as capital gains. The sale window estimates gross proceeds, withheld tax and net cash. An unpaid tax bill can now be collected in part from available cash, and tax statements show what was paid, withheld and still owed.

### Banking, bonds and funds

- **Banks closed by central bank injections were restored**
  Banks that failed or lost their charter because central bank injections charged them interest were refunded that interest and reopened.
- **Central bank liquidity injections no longer drain private banks**
  Cash a central bank injects into private banks is still owed back, but no longer charges interest. Banks can repay it at any time.
- **Greenbaum Group's bank restored**
  Greenbaum Group's bank, closed while it still held billions in government bonds, is open again and its owner has the capital they posted back.
- **A bank closed after a misleading withdraw figure is reopened**
  A player bank that failed after the withdraw page overstated its spare cash is reopened as it was, with the game covering the deposit shortfall.
- **Banks can no longer withdraw money they do not have**
  A bank's maturing treasuries were briefly counted twice, which could show far more free to withdraw than the bank really had. Withdrawals now also keep enough capital for the bank to stay healthy.
- **Government bonds held by a bank count as cash for runs and confidence**
  A bank's sovereign treasury holdings now count with its vault cash when the game scores depositor confidence and tests whether a run has broken the bank.
- **The banking page shows the savings rate you are actually paid**
  The banking page now shows the same savings rates as each bank's savings desk: central banks no longer show a much higher figure for countries with high inflation, and private banks show what a saver actually earns.
- **Bank loan payments charge each borrower their own instalment**
  A bank servicing loans to several companies in one turn could charge one company for every borrower's instalment. Each borrower now pays only its own loans.
- **Governments refinance maturing bonds instead of leaving holders unpaid**
  When a government cannot pay a maturing bond from its cash, it now sells new bonds to the market and pays every holder on time. Bonds that were stuck past maturity are paid on the next turn.
- **Refinanced bank bond repayments are paid the same turn**
  When a government refinances a maturing bond held by a bank, the money now goes straight to the bank's repayment instead of passing through the treasury, where other spending could use it first.
- **The bond market no longer creates money to refill itself**
  Each currency's bond market used to top itself up with new money every turn. It now holds only what buyers, coupons and repayments put in.
- **Retain completed bond settlement flows after a phase timeout**
  Bond processing that finishes successfully after a timeout now keeps its settlement totals, allowing the turn to continue safely without repeating payments. Interrupted or unverifiable processing still stops for repair.
- **Paying in another currency no longer strands your money**
  Buying a bond, shares or a fund with a different currency could convert your money, then fail by a rounding gap and leave you holding the foreign currency after paying the conversion fee. The conversion now always covers the price, and a purchase that fails gives the conversion back.
- **Queued index fund withdrawals pay out again**
  Index fund withdrawals waiting in the queue now pay out again instead of being skipped every turn.
- **Funds finish winding up when their sponsor is gone**
  An index fund whose sponsor corporation, or a corporation that held units in it, was deleted now finishes winding up instead of retrying every turn. Cash with no owner left goes to the fund country's central bank reserve.
- **Winding-down funds finish when a company they held is gone**
  A fund closing down could wait forever if it still held shares of a company that no longer existed. Those shares are now written off at zero so the fund can pay its holders and close.
- **Index fund deferral warning appears once**
  A fund held out of trading by queued withdrawals used to log the same "rebalance deferred" line in the turn log every hour. It now records that once, on the fund's own daily rebalance turn, until the queue clears.
- **Portfolio gain percentages are rounded**
  Holding gains and losses on the portfolio pages and the sell window now show one decimal place instead of a long raw number.
- **Portfolio history records turns with an all short-term holding**
  A large holding bought entirely within the short-term window no longer stops that turn's portfolio history from being saved.
- **Portfolio history no longer stops on large new stock holdings**
  A rounding difference on a large portfolio of recently bought shares could stop the portfolio history step for a turn. It now records normally.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Campaign Manager: every race in one place**
  A new Campaign Manager puts every race below the presidency on one page: find a race, read its map and results down to counties and constituencies, look back at the seat's past races, and boost, attack or barnstorm any candidate without leaving it. The old down-ballot election pages now open the race there.
- **House race seat margin for district elections**
  House races decided district by district now show how many votes you need for your next seat, or how many you can lose.
- **House races decided by district no longer quote a statewide quota**
  A House race where seats are decided district by district now says so, instead of describing seats as apportioned by statewide vote share.
- **Standardize General elections to 24 turns**
  Standard General election phases now last 24 turns. Longer Primary phases preserve each election's total duration and scheduled election day.
- **One-party states no longer apply vote splitting in general elections**
  General elections in one-party states no longer move votes between the ruling party and approved parties through the first-past-the-post vote-splitting step, matching how primaries already worked.
- **Bulgarian founding runoffs keep their candidates**
  A cleanup step was removing the first-round candidates of Bulgaria's founding election before its second round could open. The runoff then had nobody on the ballot, never settled, and kept the world stuck in its founding phase.
- **Prevent repeated automatic snap elections**
- **Reset confidence after a dissolution snap election**
- **Stop repeated vacancy snap Discord announcements**
  Vacancy-triggered snap elections announce their start to Discord once. Hourly processing no longer repeats the announcement for a running snap.
- **No-confidence motions end when the premier leaves office**
  A vote of no confidence now ends when the premier it names has already left office, instead of running on and cancelling the nominations to replace them.
- **Coalition chairs can nominate any coalition MP for Prime Minister**
  A party chair who also chairs a coalition can now nominate a Prime Minister from any of the coalition's parties, not only from their own party.
- **County populations persist and follow demographic changes**
  US counties now have saved populations that follow births, deaths and migration, with exact state totals. County population baselines and primary field-office maps are corrected.
- **Historical county population data for every start year**
  New worlds initialize US county populations from era-specific Census data where compatible county coverage is available, with proportional fallback for geography gaps.
- **UK constituency and Japanese prefecture populations**
- **Separate Westminster approval from constitutional stance**
  Low Westminster approval now increases independence or reunification desire regardless of Westminster's stance. High approval reduces it, with the same existing strength and cap.
- **Retired voter archetype tables removed**
  Leftover tables and screens from the retired voter archetype system are gone. Elections, approval and polls are unchanged.
- **Status bar shows when election results next update**
  The price refresh countdown in the status bar now fades every 10 seconds to a countdown for the next election results update, and back.

### Government and legislation

- **National chairs can propose through party legislators**
  National party chairs can introduce laws through a randomly selected seated party NPP. Each party can have one NPP bill in progress across all national chambers.
- **Regional legislation access and party proposals**
  Regional offices use the current legislative composer with automatic party legislator selection. Party chairs can introduce regional bills through their seated party legislators, and region headers link directly to the legislature.
- **Recognize legacy US party legislators**
  US national party chairs can propose through seated party NPPs whose older official records have no country field.
- **Explain party legislation availability**
  Party chairs can see why their party legislator proposal action is unavailable.
- **Let party legislators file articles of impeachment**
  Authorized party chairs can direct a seated NPP to file impeachment articles from legislature and executive office pages.
- **Subsidies are back in the bill builder, and governors use it too**

### Closed factories reopen when their goods run short

- Under the upcoming market, an AI company reopens a closed factory when buyers of what that factory makes are asking for far more than the market delivers.
- At most one factory per company reopens each turn, and it has to sell to stay open.

### In-house suppliers no longer look idle

- Under the upcoming market, a factory that supplies its own company's other sites counts that output as sold when it plans production.
- Such suppliers no longer cut back or close for lack of sales while their sister plants still need what they make.

### Market prices follow the orders buyers actually placed

- Under the upcoming market, each country's price for a good is set from the orders its buyers placed and the units that actually crossed its border, instead of an estimate of what trade might have happened.
- When a country's buyers cannot get enough of a good, the price they face rises, so its factories can bid for the next shipment instead of being priced out of it.

### Short goods ramp back up faster

- Under the upcoming market, a factory that sells out at a profit grows faster when buyers asked for far more than the market delivered, up to doubling its run in a turn.
- Lines cut back after a slow turn can now recover in a few turns instead of a few dozen.

### Unspent public purchasing reaches households

- Under the upcoming market, government money set aside to buy goods that are sold out is paid to households in the same country instead of being written off.
- Households then spend it on goods that are available, so the budget still reaches the companies that can supply.

### Admin tool to rebuild the economy without resetting the world

- A new admin tool can rebuild every company, market and bank from the era seed while keeping parties, laws, budgets, elections and the game calendar as they are.
- If it is ever used, every character's personal cash returns to the amount they started with, campaign funds stay, and national debt stays with each treasury.
- The tool reviews the world first and only runs against the exact plan an operator approved, and the world stays paused afterwards until it has been checked.

### Immigration laws now change how many people arrive, and a new Labour migration law

#### What changed

- Entry and residence now moves real arrivals. The most open setting brings in
  1.7 times a region's usual migrant inflow, the most selective half of it.
  Regions where more people leave than arrive are not pushed further down by a
  narrower law.
- New national law, Labour migration, for the United States, United Kingdom
  and Japan. Its five settings run from Open Labour Market Act to Work Visa
  Moratorium. Every country starts at Managed Work Visa Compact, which keeps
  today's behaviour.
- Work visas only arrive where employers want more workers than the state
  has, and more arrive the bigger the shortage. Under the two open settings
  they also arrive in states that are losing residents, which is what lets a
  short-staffed region refill. Better pay in a state draws more of them.
- The open settings raise the yearly arrival limit by the work visa intake, up
  to 3% of a region's population a year at the most open setting. Arrivals are
  mostly young working-age adults, so they add to the labour force quickly.
- Labour migration is overseen by the labour department and costs a small
  share of national output to run, more at the open and restrictive ends.

### Fewer "sending requests too quickly" errors

#### What changed

- Every feature now counts your clicks separately. Before, most features shared
  one counter per player, and each feature checked that shared count against
  its own limit. Twenty-five clicks in NPP management could then block your
  first rally, debate strategy or party action with "sending requests too
  quickly".
- NPP management (the national and state party panels) allows 120 actions a
  minute, up from 20. NPP action points remain the real limit.
- Bulk NPP actions on the national panel ("select NPPs, then run an action")
  now go out in batches of up to twenty, with one refresh at the end. A
  30-NPP run used to send 30 requests and 60 refreshes and stop partway
  through.
- Everyday campaign and party actions allow 60 a minute, up from 10 to 30.
  This covers actions, rallies and rally tours, targeted ads, field offices,
  canvassing, campaign travel, state attacks, home-state surges, GOTV,
  suppression, recruitment, registration drives, party heroes, Build Org and
  direct NPP actions. Debate strategies allow 30 a minute, up from 10, and
  abandoning a sector allows 30, up from 10.
- Login, money transfers, treasury moves and other sensitive routes keep their
  existing limits.

#### Why

Client analytics recorded 5,169 rate-limited clicks between October 2 and
October 28 from players who allow analytics, so the true total is higher. The
two party NPP management panels accounted for 63% of them, and about one in six
national NPP management attempts ended rate-limited. Campaign actions, regional
Build Org and the actions page made up most of the rest.

#### For developers

- Rate-limit keys are scoped by route or feature (`api/<route>:<userId>`). The
  new `local/no-unscoped-rate-limit-key` lint rule rejects a bare user-id key
  passed to `checkRateLimit` or `durableRateLimit`. To share a counter on
  purpose, give it an explicit prefix, as `party-influence:` does.
- New limits: `NPP_MANAGEMENT_LIMITS` (120/min) and `CAMPAIGN_ACTION_LIMITS`
  (60/min). `ELECTION_LIMITS` still covers entering, withdrawing and voting.
- `action_operation` analytics labels now cover campaign, election, NPP, party
  and sector-abandon routes that used to report `unknown`. The full list is in

### Share sales and credit lines follow real company value

#### What changed

- A lower limit only stops new borrowing. Nothing is called in early, and your
  existing loan and repayments stay as they were.
- Buying and selling shares between players is unchanged.

### Turn performance is now measured in documents written

### Governments refinance maturing bonds instead of leaving holders unpaid

#### What changed

  real treasury does: it sells a new bond to the bond market for the cash it
  lacks and pays the maturing holders in full. Index funds, NPPs, companies,
  players and banks are all paid principal on time. The bond market's own
  holding is still exchanged for new bonds rather than cash.
- Refinancing replaces maturing debt and never adds to it, so it is not limited
  by the debt ceiling. The new debt can never exceed the face of the bond being
  repaid.

  the next turn. It is never marked as a default.

  finished.

  repaid at maturity, which was blocking the estate from closing.
- Bonds already past maturity and unpaid in several countries are refinanced
  and paid on the first turn after this ships.

### AI companies stop mistaking input shortages for weak demand

AI-run companies used to judge demand by how much of a plant's full capacity it sold. A plant missing an input, for example a farm without fertilizer, can only run part of the time. It sells everything it makes, but that only adds up to a small share of capacity, so the AI read it as a market with too much supply. Those plants never grew, and AI companies kept away from the shortest markets in the game.

AI companies now look at how much of the output they put up for sale actually sold. A plant held back by missing inputs that still sells out counts as healthy demand, so it can grow and its market stays open to new AI companies. A plant left with unsold goods still counts as oversupplied, so AI companies keep holding back where buyers really are scarce.

### AI suppliers now answer every supply proposal

AI-run suppliers accept at most 10% below market price and only the volume they can spare. Before, a proposal outside that sat unanswered until you cancelled it. Now the supplier counters with the price and volume it can honour, which you can accept or counter in the Commodities tab, or declines and tells you why (state-owned supplier, embargo, or no spare capacity). The proposal form also shows the 10% limit.

### New AI companies build what is actually short

Every new AI-run plant used to open on its sector's standard product line. A new chemical plant always made mostly industrial chemicals, even when chemicals were piling up unsold and farms had no fertilizer. Goods that only a specialist product line makes, like fertilizers and pharmaceuticals, could not attract new AI companies at all.

New AI plants now compare every product line their sector can run and open on the one that makes the shortest goods, as long as it clearly beats the standard line. That only happens when one of its products is actually short. A shortage of fertilizers or pharmaceuticals now counts when AI investors decide which sector to found a company in. Product lines your era has not reached yet are never picked. If nothing is short, new plants open on the standard line as before.

This applies to worlds running the funded producer market. Older worlds are unchanged.

### One-party states no longer apply vote splitting in general elections

In first-past-the-post races, a minor party draws a small share of votes away
from the nearest major party to model vote splitting. One-party states were
meant to skip this step, since the regime already sets how the ruling and
approved parties perform. General elections still ran it. They now skip it, so
approved parties in one-party states no longer pick up that small extra share
from the ruling party.

The check reads the country's current system of government, so a country that
leaves one-party rule mid-game starts using vote splitting from its next
counted turn.

### Paying in another currency no longer strands your money

#### What changed

- When you pay in one currency for something priced in another, the game now
  converts enough to cover the full price. Before, rounding in the conversion
  fee could leave you a few units short, so the purchase failed after the
  conversion had already happened.
- Only the part you are missing gets converted. If you already hold some of
  the target currency, that is used first and you pay the fee on less.
- Position limits are checked before any money is converted, so a purchase
  that was never allowed cannot cost you a fee.
- If a purchase still fails after converting, the conversion is reversed: you
  get back exactly what you spent, fee included, and the currency you received
  is taken back. It shows in your currency history as a purchase refund.

### Plants no longer stall over a trace of construction work

Some plants need a tiny amount of construction work for every unit they make.
Construction services used to trade only inside the plant's own state, so a
plant in a state with almost no construction crews could plan a full run and
make a few percent of it. Construction services now sell anywhere in their own
country, the way freight already does, so a plant can hire crews from a
neighbouring state.

A plant that is short only of a minor input, one that makes up 5% or less of
its recipe's input cost, now runs its full plan. It improvises the missing
units and pays twice its bid price for them as an extra site cost, which goes
to the same local businesses that are paid its other site costs. A plant short
of a major input is still cut back in proportion, as before.

### Plants pay wages and site costs only for what they actually make

When short inputs cut a plant's run, its wages and site running costs now
follow what it actually produced. Idle capacity still pays its usual small
upkeep. Before, a coal plant that made under one percent of its capacity could
pay tens of thousands in wages and site costs on a few thousand of sales.

The workers reported for the plant that turn match the wages it paid, so a
starved plant reports a smaller crew. Wages that were never earned are not paid
to households, so household income is lower where plants are starved of inputs.

A plant that sells out but was short of inputs keeps its planned run next turn
and keeps bidding for inputs. Idle upkeep no longer counts against it. A plant
selling below the cost of its materials still cuts back as before.

Cash-short companies are unchanged: a run cut because the company could not
afford it was already billed for the smaller run.

### Plants that make two products follow the one that sells

#### What changed

- Some plants make several products at once. A standard chemical plant, for
  example, makes a lot of chemicals and a little plastic. Before, the plant
  decided how hard to run by adding up all of its products together. When
  chemicals piled up unsold, the plant cut its whole run, and plastic output
  fell with it, even though every unit of plastic sold.
- Now each product is planned on its own. The plant runs at the level its
  best selling product asks for, as long as the products that sell at that
  level earn enough per unit to pay the whole plant's inputs, wages and site
  costs. If they do not, the plant plans the way it did before.
- The extra units of the slow product go into the plant's stock, as unsold
  goods already did. They are offered again next turn, marked down when stock
  piles up, and slowly written down.
- Plants that make a single product are not affected, and a plant whose
  products are all piling up still cuts back as before.
- This applies to newly started economies, and to the live world once it is
  switched on there.

### Plants that sell out ramp up again after a retool or a supply shortage

After a strategy change, leftover stock of products a plant no longer makes is
still put up for sale, but it no longer holds back how much the plant runs. A
mine that sells all of its iron now grows even while old oil and gas sit unsold.

When a plant sells out but short inputs cut what it made, next turn it plans
from what it meant to make, not from the smaller amount it managed. A supply
gap no longer shrinks the plant turn after turn. This only applies while the
plant's sales cover its wages, site and input costs; a plant losing money still
cuts back as before.

### Plants that sell well keep running when the company is short of cash

- On the funded market a plant's run can now be paid out of the money that
  run earns the same turn. A company with little cash at the start of a turn
  can still run a plant whose sales cover its wages, site costs and
  materials. The amount it can borrow this way is capped at what the run is
  expected to earn, based on last turn's prices and how much of its output
  sold, with a margin for a weaker market.
- If the sales come in short, suppliers are still paid in full and the rest is
  owed to the workers and site payees as an ordinary bill, paid first out of
  the next turns' cash. No money is created.
- When a lack of cash cuts a plant's run, wages and running costs follow the
  part of the plant that actually ran. Idle capacity pays only its cold upkeep.
- A run cut by a lack of cash or materials is recorded as such, and the next
  turn plans from what the plant meant to make and how well that output sold,
  instead of treating the cut as weak demand and shrinking a step at a time.

### Plants with two products weigh only the costs that grow with output

#### What changed

- A plant that makes two or more products, such as an oil and gas well, can
  run harder for the product that sells out while the other piles up. It only
  does so when that product earns enough per unit to pay for running harder.
- That test used to count the small upkeep a plant pays on its idle
  equipment. The plant pays that upkeep whether it runs or not, so it no
  longer counts against running more. Inputs, wages and site costs that grow
  with output still count, so running harder still has to pay for itself.
- The plant still speeds up a step at a time, faster when its product is
  badly short, rather than jumping to full output at once.
- One-product plants are not affected. A plant whose products are all piling
  up still cuts back as before.
- This applies to newly started economies, and to the live world once it is
  switched on there.

### Automatic pricing now follows what buyers actually paid

  The required project rules (`custom-rules`) still run on every pull request.

#### Why

All repositories in the account share one hosted-runner pool. On busy
evenings, promotion runs waited close to an hour for a free runner behind
feature pull request jobs.

### Loans and buyouts count money the way it really is

- Bank loans and credit lines judge your income only on turns since the last
  economy reset, averaged over the turns that have actually passed.
- Your credit line limit now subtracts what you owe on bank loans, the same
  way it already subtracted credit line debt.
- Buying a player-run company now costs at least the cash and assets it
  holds, and a company founded or floated this turn cannot be bought until
  the next turn.
- When a buyout includes shares held by the stock market, the market is paid
  for them instead of the government treasury.

### Moderators can block devices from creating accounts

#### What changed

- Registration now refuses devices that moderators have blocked.

### One missed metrics refresh no longer stops the economy

On turn 561 one missed refresh left every metrics board a turn behind, and each
part of the turn that reads approval refused to run, including the company
turn. Approval now reads a board up to six turns behind, as long as the whole
country is behind together. A board from the future, or a country caught
halfway through a refresh, still fails.

### Output sent to your own sites no longer looks unsold

When a plant hands its output to your own other sectors, no sale happens, so the
sector showed "Sold 0%", no revenue and a loss. The row now adds a line such as
"100% to your sites", and the sector page shows how many units your company used
and what they are worth at your asking price. Revenue is unchanged: nothing is
paid for goods that stay inside the company.

### Portfolio history no longer stops on large new stock holdings

#### What changed

- The turn's portfolio history step allows tiny rounding differences when it
  splits a large holding into short and long term value.

### Production image installs cleanly again

The test runner was bumped to 5.0.5 while its coverage plugin stayed on 5.0.3,
which only accepts runner 5.0.3. Installs that do not read the repository's npm
settings refused the lockfile, which stopped production image builds.

### Promotions wait longer to reuse development's run

#### Why

On 2026-10-31 development's push run spent most of its 36 minutes waiting for a
shared hosted runner. The promotion stopped waiting at 35 minutes, then queued
and ran the whole suite again on the same tree.

### Sector page shows unsold output and real losses

A plant that sold none of its output could still show "100% sold last turn" and
a clearing result of x1.00 in the pricing panel. The panel now reads last turn's
actual sales: the share sold to buyers, the share sent to your own sites shown
separately, and a clearing result of x0.00 when nothing sold.

The same plant's net profit on the sector page could read as a loss of a few
dollars while it lost its whole running bill. The page now shows the profit the
turn actually booked.

Output that cannot be stored, such as electricity, and stock written down or
lost in storage are no longer counted as stock added at cost. They are gone, so
your company result now treats them as the loss they are.

### Settle income taxes before payouts

#### What changed

- Current income tax is collected with covered salary, dividend, bond-coupon and savings-interest payments.
- Eligible outstanding taxes receive partial repayments from income after current withholding, with unpaid balances retained for later payments.
- Bond principal remains protected, and already-taxed pass-through distributions avoid a second personal levy.
- Corporate receipts reserve current taxes and settle old payables before discretionary spending and distributions. Configured CEO salary is retained during an arrears-related pause.
- Required transactions and recovery receipts prevent a retried income payment from collecting or paying money twice.

- Share splits wait for pending issuance to finish. Dividend allocations stay within the corporate payout pool even when saved IPO inventory is stale.

### Stabilize corporate settlement verification

The 480-corporation comparison allows slower CI runners to finish its legacy
baseline. Its command-count and projected BSON limits are unchanged.

### Status bar shows when election results next update

Election results update on the hour and the half hour. The status bar note
beside the turn timer now alternates between the next price refresh and the
next election results update.

### Supply agreements can no longer be signed for goods that can never arrive

Local services (real estate, healthcare, construction and freight) are only
delivered to plants in the same state. It was possible to sign one for a state
where the buyer had no plant that used it, and the contract then delivered
nothing every turn with no explanation.

Proposing, countering, accepting or taking an offer is now refused when the
buyer's plants could never receive the goods, with a plain reason. When you are
the buyer, the state picker lists only states where your own plants use the
service, and the offer board says which offers your plants cannot receive
instead of offering a Take button.

Live agreements now explain a short delivery: the buyer has no plant there that
used it, the supplier produced none, the supplier produced only part of the
volume, or the buyer's plants used less than the cap.

### The hourly turn keeps running after construction moved to the country market

- A government building in several states of one country now places one
  construction order for that country instead of one per state. Duplicate
  orders stopped the turn.
- Construction work a plant carried over from before the change still names
  its old state market. The turn now accepts that stock as the country stock
  it is, instead of refusing to settle.

### Contributors disclose conflicts of interest

#### What changed

- The contributing guide has a new "Conflicts of interest" section. A
  contributor whose change could directly affect a character they play, a
  corporation they own, run or hold shares in, or anything else they control in
  game must disclose it, publicly or privately to a maintainer.
- The pull request template asks every contributor to confirm whether the
  change touches their own holdings.
- Conflicted changes are reviewed against other players' positions and are
  never merged on the author's word alone. An undisclosed conflict can lead to
  the change being reverted and in-game gains being reversed.

### Fixed a rare dividend miscalculation

#### What changed

- Fixed a rare dividend miscalculation that could overpay shareholders of a
  company with new shares still being placed on the market.
- Share structure changes wait for unfinished issuances to complete. Completed
  issuance metadata stays in step with the company's share counts.

### Freight sells to its own state before anyone else

Freight is sold in the state where it is produced. Household and company orders
now take their own state's freight offers before the wider book is cleared, so a
buyer sorted earlier in the turn can no longer buy a distant state's haulage out
from under the local demand it was meant to serve. Unsold local freight still
reaches the national market as before.

### Mines pay materials only for what they can extract

#### What changed

- Mines and wells in a state missing some of their resource mix now pay
  materials only for the share they can extract, instead of the full bill for
  resources the state cannot supply.

### Singleplayer never opens another program's database

#### What changed

- The local launcher used to assume that anything listening on its database
  port was its own database. If it was another world's, setting up a new game
  would have cleared that other world. It now uses the port only when the
  running database is the one holding this world's files, and otherwise stops
  and says another program is using the port.
- Singleplayer no longer shows the website's cookie and analytics banner. The
  local game loads no analytics, so there was nothing to agree to.

### Singleplayer saves keep working after a game update

#### What changed

- Opening a local world now applies the game's data updates that are newer
  than the build that created it, before the world is shown. Singleplayer used
  to receive none of them, so a save from an older client hit errors in a newer
  one; a 1991 save from September failed the population movement step every
  turn because its county populations were never set up.
- Creating a new local world applies the required repairs before the first
  turn and records the world as current with the build that made it. New 1991
  worlds used to miss their opening bond market pools.
- If one update cannot apply, the world still opens and the update is tried
  again next time.

### Singleplayer turns and new worlds are faster

A singleplayer turn makes thousands of small database writes, and each one used
to wait for the disk to confirm it. Your world lives on one machine, so that
wait protected nothing, and on a laptop it was a large share of the turn. Writes
are now confirmed as soon as the database has applied them and are flushed to
disk a fraction of a second later.

World setup also builds a few hundred database indexes. They now run side by
side instead of three at a time, and the database no longer samples its own
health metrics in the background.

Turns produce the same results as before, just sooner. If your computer loses
power, you can lose the last moment of play, and a turn that was running picks
up where it stopped when you reopen the world.

### Small plants that sell out recover their planned output

A plant that sold everything it made but whose crew and site bill outweighed its margin now grows from what it planned to make, not from the tiny output its staffing and resource yield happened to produce, so it no longer restarts near the minimum run every turn.

### Sold share labels state their denominator

#### What changed

- The corporation Operations summary now reads "N% of output sold", because its
  figure is sold units divided by the units the corporation produced that turn.
- The sector pricing badge now reads "N% sold of units offered last turn (all
  outputs)", because its figure is sold units divided by the units the sector
  put up for sale.
- The pricing badge tooltip states both denominators, so the two pages no longer
  look like they disagree about the same plant.

### Supply offers now say which side you are on

A row on the supply offers list showed only the other company's side, so an offer from a company selling its output looked like an offer to buy from you. Each row now states what taking it makes you, the list header reads "Their side", and the confirmation after taking an offer names the side, the volume and the company.

### The desktop singleplayer game starts again on current builds

#### What changed

- The singleplayer package recreates the hashed library names the game server
  loads. It only recognised plain names such as the database driver, so the
  error reporting library added this autumn and the file storage client were
  left out. Desktop installers drop the shortcut that hid this in local test
  runs, and a packaged build of the current game failed every request.
- Packaging now handles scoped names and requests for a file inside a library,
  and its check fails the build if any of them is missing.

### UK constituency and Japanese prefecture populations

UK constituencies and Japanese prefectures now maintain population totals through demographic changes and world resets. Their populations sum to their regions and national totals. Japanese elections also include prefecture maps of estimated vote breakdowns.

### A bank closed after a misleading withdraw figure is reopened

#### What changed

- A bank that failed after the withdraw page counted its maturing bonds twice
  and showed far more free to withdraw than it really had is reopened with its
  old charter, loans and customer deposits.
- The game covers the shortfall the bank had when it closed, and the deposit
  insurance fund gets back what it paid out for that closure.

### Banks can no longer withdraw money they do not have

#### What changed

- When a treasury bond a bank holds matures, the bank is re-valued straight
  away. Before, the repayment landed in the bank's cash while the matured bonds
  still counted on its books until the next turn, so the same money showed up
  twice. During that window the bank looked healthier than it was and offered
  far more under Free to withdraw than it really had.
- Free to withdraw now keeps the capital the bank needs to stay adequate: at
  least 8% of its loans and treasuries, and enough to pass the stress scenario.
  Before, it allowed everything above zero, so a small fall in bond prices on
  the next turn could close the bank.
- The withdraw control explains that bonds are re-priced every turn and that a
  payout close to the limit leaves little room, and the warning turns amber
  when you enter most of the limit.

### Builds that arrive in one piece no longer look stuck

#### What changed

- Capacity is built in whole plants, mines or farms. An order smaller than one
  lands all at once on its finish turn, and an order of a few plants spread over
  more turns lands one plant at a time, with turns in between where nothing
  arrives.
- The build card and the Under construction list on the sector page used to
  show a per-turn rate for these orders, so a build on schedule read as stuck
  at "0 of 0.8 in, about 0.1 a turn". They now say when the next plant lands
  if that is before the build is done, and otherwise the finish turn. The
  tooltip says whether the capacity arrives in one piece or in steps.
- Orders that land at least one plant every turn keep their per-turn rate.
  Nothing about how or when capacity is built has changed.

### Freight details on a sector are easier to read

#### What changed

- The State freight line and the one-hop freight estimate under your plants on a
  sector's overview tab now render at the normal body size, so the used and
  available capacity is legible without zooming.
- No game rules or numbers changed.

### Headquarters moves no longer wait on a construction loan

A construction loan is secured on the site it financed, and the site does not
move when the headquarters does, so the loan no longer blocks the move.

Moving to a country with a different currency still requires the secured
construction to be settled first, because the move converts the company's
currency and rewrites its sector records.

### Let party legislators file articles of impeachment

Party-sponsored articles use the existing bill-sponsorship authority and availability checks.
Presidential cases require a lower-house sponsor; governor cases require a legislator in that state.
Each voting stage now lasts 24 turns. New articles are blocked for 72 turns after the target's latest election win, including re-election. Voting thresholds, target checks and the 48-turn refiling cooldown still apply. Existing open stages retain their saved deadlines.

Votes display the filer's name and, for new NPP-sponsored articles, the requesting player's name.

New optional case attribution fields distinguish the NPP filer from the requesting player.
Existing character-filed cases remain readable without migration; the existing impeachment collection reset policy covers both.

### Output sent to your own sites no longer shows as unsold

#### What changed

- When one of your plants supplies your company's other sites, those units move
  for free: they are not sales, so they earn the supplying plant nothing, but
  the receiving sites do not pay for them. The sector page counted them as
  unsold and told you to cut your price. It now lists them separately, and the
  Unsold figure only counts units nobody took.
- The plant's idle reasons no longer blame weak demand for output that went to
  your own sites.

### Producer market resumes from its recorded opening

- A world whose plants and stock still hold an older opening generation can run

- An opening further back than the six-turn catch-up bound still stops the turn,

### Targeted embargoes now stop sales

#### What changed

- The rule that decides which sellers a buyer may buy from only ran on worlds
  with an era unit scale. On every modern world it was skipped, so an embargo
  limited to one commodity or one direction of trade had no effect on a sale.
  Only a full two-way embargo did anything, through a separate older path.
- That rule now also runs on any world with an active block embargo. Households,
  governments, investment buyers, marketing buyers and producers buying inputs
  all skip offers the embargo blocks.
- A world with no active embargo builds exactly what it built before, and era
  worlds are unchanged. Modern worlds still clear in one world market; the
  embargo filters who can buy inside it.
- A cap embargo limits volume rather than closing a route, so it does not
  switch this rule on by itself. Caps keep working on era worlds as before.

### Bond coupon tax shown under federal tax

### Bond limits no longer overstate revenue earned abroad

#### What changed

- The revenue test behind a single bond sale now converts every sector from its
  host country's currency. It used to convert all of them at the owning
  corporation's rate, so a company with sectors abroad had its revenue, and
  therefore its per-sale bond ceiling, overstated on screen and in the check
  applied when issuing.
- The debt limits themselves are unchanged; only the conversion is corrected.

### Contributor changes to the economy need a maintainer's approval

#### What changed

- Contributor pull requests must keep the conflict-of-interest section and tick
  exactly one box.
- Contributor changes to economy or turn code (markets, banking, trade, budgets,
  corporations, the turn engine) need a maintainer's approval on the current

- Contributor changes to CI workflows, code owners or the review scripts also need

### No-confidence motions end when the premier leaves office

A no-confidence motion is tied to one premier. When that seat becomes vacant
before the vote closes, the motion now ends as cancelled and its result cannot
cancel any leadership nominations opened during the vacancy.

### Bank loan payments charge each borrower their own instalment

#### What changed

- When a bank collected loan instalments from several companies in the same
  turn, the payments could all be taken from the first company. Each borrower
  is now charged exactly its own instalments.

### Page text that showed as raw labels is back

#### What changed

- The Devolution tab on the Prime Minister's office and each First Minister's
  office shows Independence Desire and Reunification Desire, last turn's
  movement, the yearly forecast, the time to the referendum threshold and both
  governments' stances again. The numbers were updating every turn, but the
  sentences that carry them rendered as raw labels.
- The same fix restores navbar-section and tutorial text wherever a page used it
  inside its own content.

#### Cause

The faster-pages release split the translation catalog in two: the site frame
sends the navbar and tutorial text, and each page adds its own. The page half
replaced the frame's text instead of adding to it, so anything inside a page that
used navbar or tutorial text lost it, for staff and players alike. The page half
now adds to the frame's text instead of replacing it.

### National corporations run their plants again

#### What changed

- National corporations and other state-owned companies hand their profit to
  the government, so they keep very little cash. Under the new market they
  could not pay wages or buy materials, and their plants sat idle.
- The owning government now advances each state company what it needs to run
  its plants for the turn: wages, site costs, materials and any bills it still
  owes. The company produces and sells like any other.
- Whatever the company does not spend goes straight back to the government the
  same turn. The government only carries what the run actually cost, and can go
  into debt to do it, as it already does when it covers a state company's
  losses.
- Workers at state companies are paid again, so their households can afford
  food and other goods.
- Private companies are not affected.

### Turns resume after a missed company production step

#### What changed

- If a turn fails before companies produce and sell, later turns no longer stop on it. Each plant resumes from its last completed production, with nothing made or sold for the missed turn.
- Construction that was due during the missed turn still arrives, and plants still age for the turns that passed, so no paid construction is lost.
- Companies founded during the missed turn join the next turn as usual.
- The recovery only covers short gaps of up to six turns. A turn whose production was recorded but never finished, or a longer gap, still stops the turn so it can be checked by hand.

### Singleplayer worlds open again after the first time

#### What changed

- The desktop app gives the local database a new port each time a world
  starts, but the database remembered the port it first ran on and refused to
  start anywhere else. The game now moves its single local database to the new
  port when it opens a world.
- The automated singleplayer check now reopens its test world on a new port,
  the way the desktop app does, so this cannot return unnoticed.
