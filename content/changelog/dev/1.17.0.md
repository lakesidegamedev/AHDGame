The headline is the tech tree and growth rework for the plants economy. Tech effects that paid out in policy-credit margin or one-time marketing bumps are remapped to plant levers (labour, inputs, output), owned nodes map automatically, and R&D pace is 20% faster. Cash prices are derived from each node's estimated per-turn value to the buying corporation times 48 turns. The R&D ecosystem modifier is deleted. `plantSectorSplit` replaces capacity capture for players and NPPs, sized and gated by the marketing strength ratio. Management overhead is a per-corporation margin charge of k x log2(plants / 300), keyed by `gameConfig.managementOverheadPpPerDoubling` (0 = off).

The rest continues the market recovery: plants pay crews and upkeep for the run they make, restart at a quarter run, and throttle loss-making output; the flat margin trim is removed; trade counts in full with exporter-priced imports; and computer-run companies stop over-building. A shadow market system runs alongside the live one for measurement only.

## Detailed release notes

### Normal businesses make a healthy profit again

- Every industry was checked at ordinary prices. Many ordinary businesses, such as hospitals, shops, factories and offices, earned too little or lost money just for existing.
- Staff costs are lower in technology, finance, healthcare, real estate, retail, construction, manufacturing, vehicles and energy.
- 23 specialised production methods use fewer materials, so they also reach a normal profit. Ordinary production methods are unchanged.
- Nobody's costs went up.

### Separate income taxes by income group

- Low Income, Medium Income, and High Income each have a separate income-tax provision and rate.
- Income-group names are consistent across countries. Existing demographic targeting remains compatible.
- Existing tier rates start at the previous income-tax rate. Budget revenue and bill estimates use income-weighted demographic shares.
- Personal salary, dividends, bond coupons, and savings interest are taxable. Starting capital, transfers, borrowing, campaign fundraising, and returned principal are exempt.
- Characters retain their starting income group; their applicable group follows recent personal earnings. Personal tax statements show gross income, tax, net income, and unpaid assessments.
- Tax collection and treasury delivery recover safely after interruptions. Unpaid tax remains a liability rather than disappearing when cash is insufficient.

### Buy a corporation and keep it as a subsidiary

- The Deals tab offers a choice between Merge and Keep as subsidiary when you make an acquisition offer.
- Keep as subsidiary pays the target's shareholders the agreed price and makes your corporation its only shareholder. Its sectors, cash, bonds, bank charter and management stay as they are.
- Subsidiary offers work for AI-run companies, which accept automatically at their asking price, and for player-run ones, which accept from their Deals tab.
- Offers are refused when they would chain subsidiaries, create circular ownership, or put one player in charge of both parent and subsidiary.
- AI-run subsidiaries no longer divest or retool their sectors on their own, so a company you bought is not dismantled behind your back.

### Campaign ground game and media upkeep rebalance

#### What changed

- Ground game and media ongoing upkeep is doubled.
- Tier 3 of every ground game and media branch costs 1.5 times as much to buy.
- Small races keep their existing budget scaling on top of this.

### Company marketing budgets on the advertising page

The Advertising page now lists company marketing budgets in a common anchor currency, with headquarters-based country and state filtering. These are configured daily budgets, not settled spending or recorded demand; funding and delivery may limit what is spent. Plant consumer rankings and other demand totals are unchanged.

### Four tech upgrades now pay off under the plants economy

Four research upgrades did little or nothing once the plants economy replaced the growth slider. Each now has a real effect, and the in-game effect labels say what it is.

- **Capacity build cost** (was growth cost): still discounts what you pay to build capacity, and now also shortens construction by 60% of the same percentage. A 20% discount means builds finish 12% sooner.
- **Output sale price** (price realization): now applies to cost-plus sectors too, adding to your markup on top of the posted price. Previously it did nothing for any sector set to cost-plus.
- **Market-dominance toll** (dominance shield): cuts the extra build cost a dominant sector pays on new capacity. This is the part of the dominance penalty that does not fade out, so the shield no longer drops to zero when the launch ramp ends.
- **Logistics strength**: besides easing the sprawl penalty, it now cuts the freight bill and the landed-price surcharge on inputs bought from other states, by up to 40% at a strength of 200. It pays off below the sprawl threshold too.

Existing caps are unchanged. The corporate R&D and logistics wiki pages describe the new effects.

### Government contract discovery and bidding windows

Eligible player CEOs receive a notification when a government tender opens, with one alert per CEO even when multiple corporations qualify. Corporation overviews include a compact link to open government bids.

Governments can choose bidding periods from 6 to 48 turns, defaulting to 24. After bidding closes, officials have another 24 turns to select a qualifying bid. Defence tenders retain their original funding and quarterly quota reservation while bidding and award decisions cross quarter boundaries. Existing tenders keep their stored deadlines.

The board explains sealed bidding, payment direction and manual award decisions. Notification links open the specific tender even when it is outside the current list page.

#### What changed

### Union pages laid out like corporation pages

- The union page opens on an Overview with the organize drive, the head's wage claim and a count of shops by strike status. Dues, services and politics, Sectors, Bargaining, Leadership and Legislative stances each have their own tab, so nothing needs a long scroll.
- Click any sector column heading to sort by it. Every heading has a tooltip that explains the column.

### AI companies stop founding businesses outside their own field

- A business outside its company's field carries a lasting margin penalty. AI companies had been opening thousands of such businesses, and about half of them lost money.
- AI companies now found only in their main or secondary field. They step outside it only for a critical shortage that shows up as real unmet demand in that market, not just a high price.
- Player companies are not affected.

### AI companies stop opening plants into full markets, and idle plants stop paying full crews

- AI-run companies now check how hard the plants already in a market are running before opening a new plant there. AI companies only open a new plant where the plants already there are selling essentially everything they can make. If they are held back by demand at all, the market is skipped, however high the price looks.
- A high price no longer lets an AI-run plant grow past the usual "run your current plant first" rule when the plants around it sit mostly idle.
- AI-run plants that have been held under a quarter of their capacity for 12 turns in a row are mothballed. A mothballed AI plant only restarts when the plants still running in that market have room to grow.
- Any plant, player or AI, that demand has held under a quarter of its capacity for 6 turns in a row now lets surplus crew go at up to 8% of the plant a turn, instead of 2%. Every other plant keeps the 2% limit.
- A new AI plant is sized to what its market is still short of. It builds for at most half of the unmet demand it can see, and skips the market when that would not fill one facility.

### Extraction mines that sell part of their output can grow into a shortage

- A mineral mine that sells only part of its output has been allowed, since the shortage-allowance change, to grow toward unmet demand in the market it sells into. For a mine in an exporting country that allowance always read as zero, because the country's own book is balanced by its exports however short its foreign buyers are.
- That gap is closed: the allowance now also counts the foreign buyers this country is not serving, at the same measured share per turn as the sold-out allowance already uses.
- Mines that sell nothing, or almost nothing, are unaffected. The change is small and gradual, and re-reads each turn what the mine actually shipped.

### Freight capacity grows faster during a critical shortage

- When freight is in critical shortage, AI-run companies whose best opportunity is logistics no longer sit out because of their cautious strategy setting.
- Those companies also get three times as many turns in which they are allowed to found a new freight site.
- Only freight and logistics change. Every other industry keeps its current pacing, and freight keeps its current pacing once the shortage is no longer critical.
- Freight prices and the inputs freight companies need are unchanged.

### Over-building a good costs sharply more, and dominant producers cannot undercut

- Capacity builds now compare what your companies could make of each good (existing capacity, builds already under way and the new order) with what the world wants of it. Up to half of world demand costs the normal price, about 3 times at full world demand and about 10 times at double. Past that the cost keeps climbing faster. Rival count and tech do not discount this charge.
- The build dialog shows a "Share of world demand" line with the multiplier and a plain explanation.
- A producer that sells more than half of a good's world supply cannot post a price below the market for that good. Negative pricing is held at the market price, for players and for AI-run companies alike.
- Capacity you already own and orders already queued are not changed.

### Plants restarting from a stop no longer run flat out for a turn with nothing to sell

- Plants sell what they made the turn before, so the first turn after a stop has nothing on the market. Until now such a plant ran at full output anyway and sold nothing.
- A restarting plant now runs at a quarter of its capacity (or more if a supply contract needs it) and grows from its real sales from the next turn.
- A plant with no crew on site hires only the crew that run needs. A plant whose crew stayed through an outage keeps the usual gradual staffing changes.

### Plants scale back when they lose money on what they make

- Every turn each plant compares what it earned from its sales with the costs it would save by making less: inputs, running costs per unit, and the part of its crew above the minimum staff.
- After six turns in a row of losing more than 5% on its sales, a plant cuts its run by 5% of its capacity each turn. After six turns of earning more than 5%, it steps back up the same way.
- One bad turn never cuts a plant, and a plant keeps a small test run going so it can tell when prices recover.
- Supply contracts with named buyers are always produced in full.
- Fewer working hours also means a smaller crew over time, so wage bills fall with the run. Upkeep on the plant itself does not change.

### Trade counts in full, and exporters can grow into foreign shortages

- Until now only half of the goods traded between countries counted toward each country's supply and demand. An exporter that shipped its whole surplus still looked oversupplied, and an importer looked shorter than it was. Trade will count in full, phased in over about a day of turns from an announced start.
- A plant that sells out and whose country exports that good now sees the foreign buyers nobody is serving, and can raise output toward them a step at a time instead of creeping up from a low run.
- A plant counts as sold out at 99% of its output (was 99.9%), so tiny leftovers no longer stop it from ramping.

### AI companies sell plants outside their industry

Each AI company can sell one plant per turn that sits outside its industry. The buyer is an AI company in that industry in the same country, and it pays book value plus the stock on hand. The plant keeps its capacity, workers and inventory. Player companies are not affected.

### Choose what the NPP running your corporation may do

#### What changed

- Four attitudes set the list in one click: Standard, Growth, Cost cutter and Advise only.
- Allowing the NPP to sell or close plants asks for confirmation first, because a plant it removes pays you nothing.
- Setting budgets or dividends to Never leaves your own settings alone instead of resetting them each turn.
- Only the CEO, or the parent corporation's CEO for a subsidiary, can change these settings.

### Extraction contracts from bidding to active concessions

### Extraction leases no longer limit how much you can dig

- Holding an extraction lease used to limit a company to its leased share of the deposit, even when nobody else was mining there. Buying a 10% lease could cut a mine's output to a tenth of the deposit.
- A lease is now a guaranteed minimum. The company keeps its reserved share, and anything it wants beyond that comes from the shared part of the deposit on the same terms as every other mine.
- Leased capacity a holder does not use still stays reserved for them.

### FX defence budgets and live policy estimates

- Live estimates show next-turn reserve draw, expected rate movement and conditional sustainability warnings while editing band or peg targets.
- Suggested bands near the current quote or fundamentals remain estimates, not guarantees.
- Supporting a currency uses foreign reserves; weakening it uses domestic funds. Both available amounts are shown separately.
- Useful impact caps the spending request. Market activity and credibility affect cost and effectiveness, and tiny peg deviations have a tolerance.
- An optional home-currency spending limit overrides the automatic limit of 10% of usable reserves per turn. Existing commitments keep their bounds.
- Paid reserve movements have recoverable publication receipts on both standalone and replicated MongoDB, with no second charge on retry.

### New plants no longer start with a full payroll

- A brand-new plant used to start with a full crew, even while it was only running part of its capacity, and then let workers go a little each turn. For its first turns it paid close to full wages for a fraction of the output.
- New plants now hire for the share of the plant that is actually running, and grow the crew as output grows.
- Plants that already have workers still lay off gradually, as before.

### Operate surplus NPC plants only as demand requires

- Use measured losses and current physical market books to qualify reversible partial operation.
- Restore full operation for named buyers, shortages, unknown books or physical constraints.
- Preserve manual player, caretaker and state-owned operating choices and the separate media policy.
- Apply the same reversible operating rule to owner-enabled autopilot corporations.

### Output your own country passes over can now sell abroad

- A country's goods were only offered abroad up to its total output minus its total demand. When buyers at home bought cheaper imports instead, the output they skipped was never offered to anyone else, so mines and plants sold less even while foreign buyers went short.
- That skipped output is now offered to foreign buyers who still need it, at the usual delivered price, with tariffs and embargoes applied as before.
- Turns where home buyers took their own country's goods, or where nobody abroad is short, are unchanged.

### Prices let go of old shortages

- Some materials, such as freight, coal, energy and steel, stayed expensive long after their shortages eased, because prices kept a memory of past shortages.
- In two steps, prices keep only three quarters and then half of that extra memory. Prices still rise with inflation and with real shortages today.
- Each step is checked after it goes live and switched back if it causes problems.

### Regional employment and legislative job matching

#### What changed

- Partial corporate coverage no longer repeatedly increases unemployment.
- Hiring, layoffs, labor-force changes and business ownership transfers use employment counts across all countries.
- Existing national and regional laws are recognized without passing them again. Program effects respect implementation and funding.
- Existing worlds adjust gradually. Additional operating jobs and effective policy can bring unemployment below 5%.

### Removing the flat fee on every business

- Since 10 October every business has paid a flat fee of about 8 parts in every 100 of its income. It was added when profits were much higher than they are now.
- Removing it raises every business's profit margin by about 8 points from the turn it takes effect.
- It is switched on as one step of the market recovery, checked the turn after, and switched back if anything goes wrong.

### Well-run state companies are as efficient as private ones

- State-owned companies used to lose at least 5 points of margin just for being state-owned, and most lost far more.
- That flat penalty is gone. A state company in a clean, well-governed country with no mandates now runs like a private one.
- Corruption, poor governance, price controls, guaranteed jobs and nationalising too much of the economy still reduce state company margins, up to the same limit as before.

### Companies hire faster

- Companies now fill open jobs within a few turns when people in the state are looking for work. Before, only a small part of a company's open jobs was filled each turn, so plants stayed short of workers even where unemployment was high.
- Hiring is still limited by how many people are looking for work, so a state with very low unemployment can still run short.

### Entertainment services removed

- Entertainment services are no longer a traded commodity. Households and governments no longer buy it, and its share of household spending now goes to the other goods households buy.
- Entertainment media and streaming, cable and publishing strategies now sell advertising instead. Their capacity is adjusted once so each plant's output is worth the same as before.
- Planned economies' media now sells advertising, bought by their own state media budget.
- Countries no longer found new firms because of an entertainment shortage.

### AI-run companies shrink unused plants faster

#### What changed

- AI-run companies with a plant that is selling well below its capacity now begin shrinking it after 2 turns instead of 6, and cut 15% of the unused part each turn instead of 4%.
- AI-run plants that sell everything they make but still run far below capacity now begin shrinking after 6 turns instead of 24.
- AI-run companies no longer add new capacity in a sector where plants are running below 90% of capacity because of weak demand. Replacing worn-out capacity is unaffected.
- Player-owned and autopilot plants are unchanged.

### Bank network capacity is shown separately

Financial sector pages now show the capacity reserved for your bank branch network instead of listing it under Other limits. The explanation shows your charter's branch capacity share and how that capacity backs your bank's deposit ceiling.

### Plants pay wages and upkeep for the part that is running

- A plant held below full output by weak demand now staffs and pays wages for the run it is making, with a minimum crew of a quarter of its active capacity so it is never staffed at zero.
- Crews shrink gradually, by at most 2% of the plant each turn, so there are no sudden mass layoffs. When demand comes back, plants hire back straight away.
- Job counts in a state follow the same rule, so a glutted plant no longer asks the local labour market for workers it is not using.
- Capacity left idle because buyers would not take the output now carries about a third of the usual idle upkeep. Overbuilding still costs something.
- Capacity you idle yourself with the production slider, and mothballed capacity, are billed as before. A plant running at full output is billed exactly as before.

### Retail bank charters stop charging full staffing for branch capacity

Financial sectors with an active deposit-taking charter now charge labour and maintenance on the capacity that actually produces financial services, instead of the whole sector. The capacity reserved for the branch network is still fully staffed, and the bank's deposit business is what covers it, so running branches no longer forces the sector into a structural loss on its own. Sectors without an active branch charter, and worlds with private banking switched off, are unchanged.

- There is no editor yet. The settings page follows.

### Connect poverty outcomes to funded laws

#### What changed

- Existing national and regional poverty-law effects now reach the economy engine through their paid implementation factors. Missing, expired, future and pre-enactment funding do not provide relief.
- Unreplaced opening statutes retain their funded relief. A replacement, including leaving a federal family to the states, suppresses its opening family instead of stacking both versions.
- Relief has diminishing returns and replaces legacy booked social spending for v2 legislation, avoiding a second bonus for the same programs. Employment and active Cabinet effects retain their separate channels.
- Unattributed historical adjustments halve over 48 turns instead of remaining indefinitely. Existing values transition gradually rather than being reset.
- Predetermined political-board crime inputs reach the poverty calculation instead of falling back to a constant.
- Poverty is labeled as a modeled population-share estimate, with population-weighted national aggregation. It is not a surveyed household rate or an official country-specific poverty threshold.

### Federation breakups and Russian reforms now get a real vote

- The Soviet Congress can now open the negotiated dissolution settlement by itself in the 1991 world, as long as no player holds a seat in it or runs the government. Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia already could.
- Settlements opened by the government are now sponsored by the governing party, and a settlement opened by a seated deputy is sponsored by that deputy's party. Before this, deputies had no party line to follow and abstained, so the bill failed without a single vote cast.
- Czechoslovakia's 1992 settlement failed that way. Its Federal Assembly will now table it again for a real vote.
- Russia's post-Soviet decisions on the presidency, the Federal Assembly, the Duma electoral law and the Federation Council are now sponsored by the governing party, so they reach a real vote as well.
- Each republic still has to ratify the same terms before a federation dissolves.
