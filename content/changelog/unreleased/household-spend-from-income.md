## What changed

- Households used to try to spend 90% of all the cash they held, every turn.
  Money they could not spend because shelves were empty stayed in their
  wallets and was ordered again the next turn, on top of new wages. Orders grew
  to about four times what households could really spend, so every shortage
  looked much worse than it was and prices were pushed up harder than needed.
- Behind a new switch, off until it is turned on, households now spend 90% of
  this turn's wages plus 30% of their savings. Orders match real spending
  power, and savings are spent over several turns instead of all at once when
  shelves refill.
