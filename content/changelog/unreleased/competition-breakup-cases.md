title: Competition authorities can now break up dominant groups
summary: Where the Competition Act is at structural enforcement or higher, a dominant group can be ordered to sell down.
tags: [legislation, corporations, antitrust]
badges: [minor]
areas: [fullstack]
---

- In a country whose Competition Act is at Structural Enforcement or Open Markets Charter, a group that holds more of a national industry than the act allows (half, or 40% at the top level) gets a competition case.
- The minister who reviews mergers has 6 turns to order a breakup or dismiss the case, from the company's page. If they do not decide, the case follows the measured share: still above the limit means a breakup order.
- A breakup order gives the group 12 turns to bring its share below the limit. Until then it cannot make new acquisitions, and once late it is fined each turn, the same as a merger remedy.
