Energy sectors have four new ways to make power.

- **Hydroelectric** is open to every energy company in every era. It is clean
  and nearly as steady as fossil power, and cheap to run, but each site makes
  a little less power. Its inputs are building materials, steel and
  construction work.
- **Gas-Fired** is also open from the start. It is firm power with about half
  the emissions of coal, so carbon prices hit it half as hard and coal
  phase-out laws leave it alone. Its running cost follows the natural gas price.
- **Wind Power** and **Solar Power** replace Renewables Focus. The Renewables
  R&D research node unlocks both; solar opens from 2009. Both are clean, both
  lose output when they supply most of a region's power without storage, and
  they lean on different inputs: wind on steel and rare earth, solar on
  electronics.

Hydro, wind and solar count toward a clean power standard. Conventional power
that retires in 2039 can convert at the discount to advanced fission, wind,
solar, gas-fired or hydroelectric.

Any sector still on Renewables Focus moves to Wind Power, and any subsidy that
targeted renewables now covers both wind and solar.
