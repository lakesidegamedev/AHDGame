## What changed

- **Doctrine for the 2050s.** The doctrine tree now has a 2050s era with new doctrines for maneuver, fires, defence, logistics, cyber and surveillance. Generals can train new traits from the 2020s, 2030s and 2040s, such as counter-drone defence, drone network command and autonomous logistics.
- **New formations.** Counter-drone battalions and loitering munition wings from 2027, autonomous naval drone flotillas and space sensing constellations from 2030, and directed energy air defence wings from 2040. Each needs modern materiel in the national arsenal, which the country's own defence industry must research and deliver first. They also need trained personnel, money from the defence budget and ongoing upkeep.
- **Cyber resilience.** Every modelled country now has a cyber resilience score from 0 to 1. It rises with a stronger Cyber Resilience Mandate, with research spending and with the state of the utilities. A higher score makes infrastructure cyberattacks less likely.
- **Treaty organisations after 2027.** The Critical Minerals Club (2028), the Climate Adaptation Finance Pact (2029), the AI Safety Accord (2030), the Arctic Agreement (2032) and Space Traffic Rules (2034) open empty in their year and countries can join. Each has its own treaty terms that members vote in. The terms run for five years and then lapse unless the members renew them.
- **Media models.** Social platforms (the established model in 2027), creator networks, AI-generated media from 2030 and immersive media from 2040 join the media operating models, each with its own running costs and a research step that unlocks it.
