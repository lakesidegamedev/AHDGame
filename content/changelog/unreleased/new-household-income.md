  Households in a growing country now receive a little new money each turn,
  sized by real GDP growth and spent like the rest of their money, so a
  closed economy can grow. It favours no company, sector, good or player.

Household spending came almost entirely from the wages plants paid, so total
demand could only grow as fast as the wage bill, and the economy could not
grow on its own.

- **How much.** Each turn, households in a country get new income equal to a
  share of their own income (wages and government transfers). The share
  follows the country's real GDP growth and stops while the economy shrinks.
- **No winners.** Every household in a country gets the same share of its
  income. The money is spent through the normal household market, on the same
  goods and by the same rules as all other household money. No company,
  sector, good or player is paid directly.
- **On screen.** The central bank's money supply tab shows the new household
  income created last turn and since the world began, and it is part of
  household cash.
- **Checked every turn.** Each turn compares what it planned to pay with what
  was actually created, and flags any difference.
