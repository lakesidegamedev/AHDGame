title: "Party nominees carry their party's name recognition"
summary: >-
  A major party's presidential nominee no longer loses votes in every state
  because they were personally less known than their rival.
tags: [elections, president, balance]
badges: [patch]

In a presidential general election, a candidate's national name recognition
scaled their vote in every state at once. A nominee who won their party's
primary with little national profile of their own was multiplied down
everywhere, so a gap in recognition could turn a close race into a 50-state
landslide, even in states that lean heavily toward the nominee's party.

The nominee of a major party now carries the party's recognition onto the
ballot: their name recognition counts as at least 25 in the general election.
Candidates better known than that keep their full advantage. Minor parties and
independents still run on their own profile. A party counts as major when it
holds at least 15% of national party registration.

The change applies to ballots cast from now on in the current race.
