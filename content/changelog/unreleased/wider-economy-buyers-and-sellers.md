Every state in a country with company plants now has a small "rest of the
economy" that trades each good except ordnance. Its size is about a tenth of
normal demand, split by each state's share of real economic output, and it grows
only with real growth.

It never competes with a company:

- **As a seller**, it only fills orders that no company could fill, for buyers in
  its own country, a little above the normal price. A company's output always
  sells first. Plants short of an input can count on it when they plan their run,
  so a shortage of something like advertising no longer has to cut output.
- **As a buyer**, it only takes output that no one else bought, never above the
  normal price.

What it sells counts as supply only for what was actually sold, and its sales
never set a market's clearing price. The money it takes in and pays out leaves
and enters through its own account each turn, so it creates no lasting money.
