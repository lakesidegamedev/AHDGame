## What changed

- The head of intelligence can launch a cyber operation against another country from the intelligence console routes. It costs money from the intelligence appropriation, like other covert action.
- Success depends on your cyber strength and your service's tradecraft against the target's cyber resilience, which comes from its resilience mandate, research spending and utilities. A better defended target is harder to hit.
- A successful operation causes a Cyber Attack crisis on the target: grid outages, lost output and growth, and a response decision for its head of state. A failed one does nothing to the target.
- Separately, the target may trace the operation to its sponsor, whether it worked or not. When it does, world tension rises, the news names the sponsor, and the sponsor's government loses approval for a few turns.
- Operations between the same two countries need a long gap between them.
- Either government can publicly stand down. That opens a truce that blocks operations between the pair in both directions for a long stretch, and eases world tension.
- In a 2027 world, hawkish background governments can probe the leader of the rival bloc when the odds favour them, and stand down when they turn dovish or the world cools.
- Other start dates are unchanged.
