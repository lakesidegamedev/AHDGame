## What changed

- Background nation pages explain that the credit rating comes from debt to GDP alone, and that a bond's coupon is fixed when it is issued, so a AAA country can still pay a high coupon.
- Party lines show seats, share of seats and share of votes separately.
- Country briefs say their bond price is the price on the day they were written. Rating upgrades now get their own story instead of a debt strain headline.
- Election and outlook stories mention the country's own economy, so they no longer read as the same text.
- The wire reports a government's approval when it moves 5 points or more in one turn.
- The US country page uses one approval figure everywhere, counts 50 states and DC, and says the history chart ends at the last recorded turn.
