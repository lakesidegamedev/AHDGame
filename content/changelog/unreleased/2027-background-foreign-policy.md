## What changed

- A background country's ruling parties now decide its stance abroad. Statist and nationalist governments raise tariffs, market liberals lower them. EU members keep the common EU tariff.
- Outside the EU, a protectionist and hawkish government can put an extra tariff on goods from the leader of the rival bloc. Tariffs move a little at a time, never all at once.
- Governments lean their country toward or away from a bloc over the years. A country that leans far enough can apply to join an alliance, and one that drifts away can leave, through the same rules every other country follows.
- When a country goes to war against one of its partners, background governments can sanction it. Sanctions last half a year, are only renewed while the war goes on, and are lifted early when the war ends or a more dovish government takes over.
- When an alliance calls its members to a war they are not bound to join, a background government decides once. Weak or dovish governments say no, and a refusal stands. Background countries never start wars of their own.
- In living conflicts, background governments now answer the world's call: hawks commit most, doves least, and the rest take the usual course.
- A background country that sells more abroad than it buys sees its currency strengthen over time, and one running a deficit sees it weaken.
- From 2027 the Cold War tension console also tracks tension between each pair of blocs: the US-led bloc, the China-led bloc and the non-aligned countries. Wars and sanctions between two blocs raise their pair, and readiness answers to whichever is hotter, the world's overall tension or the hottest pair.
- Worlds started from any other date, and the live world, behave exactly as before.
