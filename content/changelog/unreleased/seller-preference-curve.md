## What changed

- Added a price preference for the producer market. When it is on, a buyer with a free choice of sellers splits its order across every seller it can buy from instead of buying only from the cheapest one. This covers household, business and public purchases of world-market goods.
- Cheaper sellers still sell the most. A seller priced 5% above the cheapest keeps a solid share, one 10% above sells about half as much per unit, and one 30% or more above sells little.
- A small price cut now wins more sales, not all of them. Sellers who are slightly dearer can still win buyers back by narrowing the gap, without having to match the lowest price exactly.
- Buyers never pay more than their price limit or spend more than they have. Each seller is still paid its own posted price, and named suppliers, contracts, a company's own sites and buying from your own country first work as before.
- A later step can also let brand loyalty and product quality nudge a seller's share up or down.
- The rule is switched off until it is enabled, so nothing changes for players yet.
