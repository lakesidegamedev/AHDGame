## What changed

- During a second pandemic you can order a lockdown or keep the economy open. A lockdown protects public health but costs jobs, strains lenders and lifts borrowing costs. Staying open protects jobs for now, lets more people fall ill, and hurts the government at the next election.
- Finance ministers can issue emergency health bonds, and central bank chairs can backstop lenders and bond markets.
- During an AI labour shock you can bail out the hardest-hit sectors (jobs held, bond yields up) or push structural reform (jobs worse first, voters angry, better footing later). Finance ministers can fund a wage subsidy and central bank chairs can allow lender forbearance.
- Each choice shows its tradeoff on the crisis screen, spends action points and treasury or political capital like any other crisis response, and lands in the crisis record that decides blame afterwards. The effects hit your own country only.
