## What changed

- New product: Consumer goods (furniture, appliances and household items). Manufacturing plants can switch to the new Consumer Goods recipe, which uses plastics, timber, chemicals, electronics, steel and power. Households now spend part of their budget on it.
- Retail sectors are now stores. A store makes nothing. It buys groceries, consumer goods and electronics from producers at the market price and sells them to households in its own state at cost plus a markup. Stores never sell to companies or governments, and never sell weapons or business goods.
- The store markup is 35%. When stores first open it starts at 40% and comes down to 35% over 4 turns, so retail companies move over gradually instead of losing their old income in one turn. The markup always stays between 10% and 40%.
- A store pays wages, site bills and running supplies only for the share of its capacity that its sales need. The rest of the store stands idle and pays only its upkeep, so a large store in a state with few shoppers no longer runs at a loss on capacity it cannot use.
- Households spend 60% of their food money, all of their consumer goods money and 70% of their electronics money in stores. Cars, medicines and everything else are bought direct from producers, as before.
- A store can only resell as much as its capacity allows each turn, and the share of household spending sent to stores is worked out from what households actually spend at market prices, so a full store is filled. If a state has too few stores, households buy the rest direct from producers at the producer price with no markup, so producers of food, electronics and consumer goods do not lose sales because stores are missing.
- A store's revenue is the full value of the goods it sold to households. What it paid producers for them is its own line, Cost of goods resold, and the markup is its gross profit. The store page shows revenue, cost of goods resold (by product), markup, running costs and profit, and the lines add up. It also shows how much of the store's capacity its sales used. Company financials and country statements show the same Cost of goods resold line. Market pages for food, electronics and consumer goods show how much was sold through stores and how much direct. The build screen describes stores instead of a product they make.
- NPP companies open new stores and grow existing ones in states where households want more store sales than the stores there can serve.
- The old Retail baskets product is gone. Its stock, prices and open listings are cleared, and existing retail sites keep their owner, size and location and become stores. Stores now buy only what it takes to run them (power, freight, advertising, software and similar), not the goods they resell.
- Plants now buy fewer kilowatt-hours and more raw goods: steel and vehicle plants use more iron and coal, building materials and construction use more timber, chemical and plastics plants use more gas and oil, farms use more fertilizer, and freight uses oil. Each recipe keeps the same total input cost, so margins stay about the same.
