## What changed

- Each country keeps a price index that starts at 1 and follows its inflation rate every turn. National and regional GDP are now current-price figures: real output times that index.
- Reported GDP growth is unchanged and stays the real annual rate. Capital, productivity and the output gap still run on real output.
- Over decades, debt-to-GDP, revenue-to-GDP and deficits no longer drift just because prices rose. Before, tax bases and debt grew with inflation while GDP did not.
- Every budget figure that divides by GDP (debt ratio, spending envelopes, deposit returns, the inflation fiscal term) now uses one GDP, refreshed and smoothed every turn instead of once a year. Spending envelopes sized on GDP will sit slightly lower than before because the smoothed figure trails the live one.
