summary: >-
  A company buying shares or fund units with its own cash now warns you before
  the purchase leaves too little to pay its plants, and never lets it spend
  money it does not have.

Building capacity already stopped a company from spending the cash its plants
need to run. Buying on the share market did not, so a large share purchase
could leave a company unable to pay wages and inputs, and every plant would sit
idle the next turn.

Now the same protection covers every way a company spends its own cash on the
share market: buying shares at market, placing a buy order, filling someone
else's sell order, making an offer on a private listing, buying fund units, and
a hostile takeover. If the purchase would leave less than three turns of your
plants' running costs in cash, you see how much you would have left, how much
your plants need, and the most you can spend while keeping that reserve. You
can still go ahead by pressing Buy anyway.

A company can never spend more cash than it has on the share market, even if
you confirm.

Buy orders you leave on the book are also covered. If your company's cash has
dropped below its reserve by the end of a turn, your newest company buy orders
give back as much of their held cash as the reserve needs before any of them
fill. The order shows how much it gave back and why. An order you placed by
pressing Buy anyway is left alone.

Buying shares with your own personal cash is unchanged.
