---
date: 2026-10-30
title: Plants pay wages and site costs only for what they actually make
summary: >-
  A plant whose inputs ran short no longer pays a full crew and full site bill
  for output it never made. It pays for the share it produced, plus the small
  upkeep on idle capacity.
tags: [economy, corporations]
badges: [patch]
areas: [engine]
---

When short inputs cut a plant's run, its wages and site running costs now
follow what it actually produced. Idle capacity still pays its usual small
upkeep. Before, a coal plant that made under one percent of its capacity could
pay tens of thousands in wages and site costs on a few thousand of sales.

The workers reported for the plant that turn match the wages it paid, so a
starved plant reports a smaller crew. Wages that were never earned are not paid
to households, so household income is lower where plants are starved of inputs.

A plant that sells out but was short of inputs keeps its planned run next turn
and keeps bidding for inputs. Idle upkeep no longer counts against it. A plant
selling below the cost of its materials still cuts back as before.

Cash-short companies are unchanged: a run cut because the company could not
afford it was already billed for the smaller run.
