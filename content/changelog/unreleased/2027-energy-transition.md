## What changed

- In a 2027 world, renewables lose up to 15% of their output in regions where they supply most of the power and nothing firms them. Grid storage plants, and smart grids at half strength, remove the loss once there is about one unit of storage for every four of renewables.
- Power plants older than 40 years and factories older than 30 cost more to run each year past that age, up to 20% more. Building new capacity brings a fleet's average age down. Opening fleets start with a realistic age, so old coal power starts to feel it around 2040.
- Three new laws can be filed from 2027 in the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, China and Ireland:
  - **Carbon Price**: coal and gas power, chemical works, factories, mines and builders pay a charge on their carbon. In the United States and United Kingdom the charge also raises budget revenue.
  - **Coal Phase-Out**: sets a closure date for coal power. Running costs for coal plants climb over the ten years before the date.
  - **Clean Power Standard**: requires a share of national power to be clean. Coal and gas plants pay for the shortfall when the country misses it.
- In a 2027 world these laws also lower the emissions that drive climate pressure, so a country that passes them helps slow the warming everyone shares.
- The top two levels of the critical minerals law now include a recycling duty that recovers a tenth or a fifth of the rare earths firms use.
- From 2035 in a 2027 world, surveying a deposit costs more the more of it has already been mined, up to three times as much for a worked-out field.
- In other worlds the new laws can still be passed and move the usual political measures, but the costs on firms, plant ageing and storage effects stay off.
