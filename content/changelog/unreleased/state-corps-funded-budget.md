## What changed

- National corporations and other state-owned companies hand their profit to
  the government, so they keep very little cash. Under the new market they
  could not pay wages or buy materials, and their plants sat idle.
- The owning government now advances each state company what it needs to run
  its plants for the turn: wages, site costs, materials and any bills it still
  owes. The company produces and sells like any other.
- Whatever the company does not spend goes straight back to the government the
  same turn. The government only carries what the run actually cost, and can go
  into debt to do it, as it already does when it covers a state company's
  losses.
- Workers at state companies are paid again, so their households can afford
  food and other goods.
- Private companies are not affected.
