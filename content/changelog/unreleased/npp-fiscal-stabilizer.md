## What changed

- Countries run by AI governments could run a primary deficit of more than 10% of GDP for years, because the only response was the occasional budget bill.
- At the end of each fiscal year, an AI government with a primary deficit beyond 3% of GDP now enacts an austerity budget. When debt is high and still rising, it aims for a balanced primary budget instead.
- Each year's package closes about a third of the gap, and at least 2% of GDP or the whole gap if smaller, so a deficit of around 11% of GDP closes within five years.
- Left-leaning governments lean on tax rises, right-leaning governments lean on spending cuts, and centrist governments split it evenly. Tax rises arrive gradually, a point at a time, like any other tax change.
- No spending area is cut by more than 15% in a year or below half its normal level, and defence and debt interest are never cut.
- Austerity costs the government a little approval, more for a bigger package, fading over a year. It appears as an "Austerity budget" item on the approval page, and each package is announced in the news.
- When debt interest takes more than half of government revenue for two fiscal years in a row, the country restructures its debt through the existing sovereign debt crisis rules: bondholders take a haircut, the country is locked out of bond markets for a while, and then it recovers.
- Countries run by players are unchanged.
