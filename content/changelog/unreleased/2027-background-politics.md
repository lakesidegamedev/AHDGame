## What changed

- Each background country has a ruling party or coalition, a named head of government and an approval rating. Leaders age, retire and are replaced by their party.
- Approval follows the economy: growth above the country's normal pace helps, while high inflation and rising unemployment hurt. Long-serving governments slowly lose favour.
- A national election for the lower chamber is held on each country's own term, with seats shared out by that country's voting system. A government whose approval stays very low for half a year faces an early election.
- New parties can appear when part of the political spectrum has been left empty for two elections, or as a protest against a very unpopular government. Parties stuck under 2% for two elections merge into their nearest neighbour.
- National budgets now come from each economy: tax take is a share of national output and spending covers public programmes plus interest on the debt. When the deficit gets out of hand the government passes an austerity package, and its approval pays for it. Left-leaning governments lean on taxes, right-leaning ones on cuts.
- Their deficits keep issuing government bonds in their own currencies, which American players can buy like any other country's bonds.
- Each background country has one national index fund that tracks its output and profits. Players can buy and sell it like any other index fund.
- Worlds started from any other date, and the live world, behave exactly as before.
