## What changed

- The 2027 globe has its own look instead of the 1991 one. Sunlight falls as it will at the first turn, so the night side is dark with city lights, and routes link the six economies that run the full simulation and the larger background ones, with data moving along them.
- A busier sky: a low constellation of small satellites joins the relay and the satellites that beam down to the year's headlines.
- Canada is labelled beside the United States, and Quebec is outlined as the border a referendum could create.
- The tier key under the globe shows only the three tiers a 2027 world has, with their counts: 2 player nations, 4 computer-run, 16 background.
- A clock above the headline shows when the world opens (Sunday 15 November, 5:00 PM Eastern) and when the first turn runs (Monday 16 November, 5:00 PM Eastern), counting down to each. It reads the live game settings and drops each time once it passes.
- Links under the headline to the 2027 world overview and the November 2026 State of the Game post.
- The carousel shows six 2027 cards (automation and work, an ageing population, climate pressure, cyber operations, Canada and Quebec, and the calendar to 2050) instead of 1979 leaders.
- The idle globe tour adds the megadrought and climate migration.
- The country list groups 2027 countries by tier, and the feature list names Canada's House of Commons, provincial legislatures and the Canadian dollar.
- Computer-run countries are labelled "Computer-run" instead of "Soon" in every era.
- Reduced motion keeps the globe still: no moving data, satellites parked.
