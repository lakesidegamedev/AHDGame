## What changed

- The World table shows the head of government of background nations instead of Vacant. Country briefs show the date they were written.
- Every country list says how many nations it counts and which. Rankings state how the overall score is calculated.
- Leaderboards say which of a player's lives each row ranks, and both boards break ties the same way. Ranking options use readable names.
- Trend chips show percent change. Japan's median income previously read as a raw change.
- Countries show their number of national policy positions instead of calling them laws.
- Organization dues totals include every member that pays. Votes explain who can vote and why allies often vote alike. The executive tab is now Candidate endorsements.
- Foreign affairs says which diplomacy exists in the game, and explains a greyed-out war list.
- Defence pages say which units each count covers. Old defence and military links redirect.
- Scripted crises show a severity based on their effects. Resolved crises show when they began.
- Central bank, stock market and accounting unit figures say what they measure.
- The IMF company shows a player description.
- The character bar, navigation and Ask now follow the same active character.
