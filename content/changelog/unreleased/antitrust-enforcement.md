title: Subsidiary and foreign investment laws now bind
summary: Groups over a country's limits must give up control within 48 turns, or their stake is sold at book value.
tags: [legislation, corporations, antitrust]
badges: [minor]
areas: [backend]
---

- If a country's Subsidiary Limits Act caps groups below what yours runs there, you cannot formalize, spin off or acquire another subsidiary in that country.
- If a country's Foreign Investment Act bans foreign-controlled subsidiaries, a group headquartered abroad cannot take control of a company there by acquisition, takeover or share purchase.
- When a law tightens, or foreign groups end up holding more of an industry than the law allows, the affected group gets a notice and 48 turns to sell, spin off or release the company. After that, its controlling stake is sold down to half at book value on the stock market, and the group is paid for it.
