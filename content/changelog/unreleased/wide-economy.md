## What changed

- When it is switched on, each US and Canadian state gets a buyer standing in for the rest of its economy. For every good that had spare supply when it starts, it buys up to the gap between what buyers wanted and 10% more than what was on offer. Goods that were already short get nothing from it.
- It never pays more than the normal market price, so it steps aside whenever a good runs short, and its unfilled orders are never counted as demand, so they never show up as a shortage or move a price.
- Its size is fixed when it starts and then grows only with each state's real economic growth, never with how much companies make or with prices.
- It buys through the same markets households reach, so where a plant is built does not decide who sells to it.
- It ships switched off and changes nothing until it is turned on.
