## What changed

- From 2035, legislatures can pass a climate adaptation fund, a managed retreat programme for homes on coasts and floodplains that can no longer be defended, a universal basic income pilot, rules on human gene editing, and a space traffic act for crowded orbits.
- From 2040 they can also pass an automation dividend, a four-day work week, a central bank digital currency as legal tender, and a sovereign wealth fund.
- Every law has winners and payers. Managed retreat protects people from floods but costs homeowners and old coastal towns. A basic income pilot gives security but some people work less. A four-day week gives families time but costs employers. Digital currency makes payments cheaper but every payment leaves a record.
- In the United States and Britain, the automation dividend raises a levy and pays it back out to households, a digital currency earns the state a margin once it is legal tender, and a sovereign wealth fund pays part of its returns into the budget.
- Tying the pension age to life expectancy now cuts pension spending in the United States and Britain. A periodic review costs a little to run; each stronger level saves more.
- From 2040 the automation tax closes to new bills, because the automation dividend replaces it. In Japan, the older work culture reform law also closes to new bills that year, when the four-day week opens. A law already in force stays in force with its costs and effects.
- All nine new laws start with no statute in force, so nothing changes until a bill passes.
