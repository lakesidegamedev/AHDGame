## What changed

- In countries run by computer politicians, the same parties used to hold power for decades. Nothing new was ever founded and nothing ever went away.
- After each national general election, a party that has governed two terms in a row with approval under 40%, or held more than 55% of the national chamber for two terms, may split. The faction forms a new party next to its parent on the political map, takes about a quarter of the parent's politicians (those closest to its views) and keeps any seats they hold.
- When part of the political map has had no party within reach for two elections, a new party may be founded there with a small group of politicians in one region.
- A party under 2% of the national vote in two general elections in a row folds into the closest party. Its politicians, seats and offices move with it.
- At most one split and one new party per country per election. None of this is guaranteed: whether and when it happens depends on approval, seats and votes.
- A party holding more than half the seats no longer gets a bigger incumbency boost for every extra seat it holds.
- A party that held a region in either of the last two election cycles may recruit there again, even if it was wiped out.
- Player-created parties never split or fold this way. Worlds without the switch are unchanged.
