title: Federation breakups and Russian reforms now get a real vote
summary: Dissolution settlements for the Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia, and Russia's post-Soviet constitutional bills, are now put to an actual vote instead of failing because every deputy abstained.
tags: [world, ussr, succession, legislature]
badges: [patch]
areas: [backend]
---

- The Soviet Congress can now open the negotiated dissolution settlement by itself in the 1991 world, as long as no player holds a seat in it or runs the government. Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia already could.
- Settlements opened by the government are now sponsored by the governing party, and a settlement opened by a seated deputy is sponsored by that deputy's party. Before this, deputies had no party line to follow and abstained, so the bill failed without a single vote cast.
- Czechoslovakia's 1992 settlement failed that way. Its Federal Assembly will now table it again for a real vote.
- Russia's post-Soviet decisions on the presidency, the Federal Assembly, the Duma electoral law and the Federation Council are now sponsored by the governing party, so they reach a real vote as well.
- Each republic still has to ratify the same terms before a federation dissolves.
