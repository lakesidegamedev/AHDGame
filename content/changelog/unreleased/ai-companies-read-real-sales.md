## What changed

- Computer-run companies used an older market estimate of how much each plant sold. That estimate often disagreed with the sales plants were actually paid for. They now read the real sale.
- This drives their wage settings, output policy, which glutted plant they mothball, whether a company pivots its strategy, mine recipe switches and the glut discount on supply contracts between computer-run companies. The thresholds are the same as before.
- Companies on autopilot follow the same rules, so the autopilot "pivot" advice now reflects real sales too.
- A plant with no sale on record for the latest turn (new, mothballed, or offering nothing) counts as no evidence either way, not as a plant that sold nothing.
- Brand loyalty is now scored from what each company really sold. Pricing posture no longer counts, since the market has no posted posture. Media companies are still scored on advertising sales. Loyalty moves at most about a point a turn, so scores drift rather than jump.
- Plants now keep a count of consecutive turns selling less than half of what they offered. Computer-run companies only sell off a stranded plant once that count reaches 12, so no plant is sold off this way for at least 12 turns after this update.
