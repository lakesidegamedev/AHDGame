title: Plants that sell well keep running when the company is short of cash
summary: >-
  A company that spent its cash on construction no longer has to idle plants
  whose sales would pay for their own run. Wages, site costs and materials can
  be paid out of that turn's sales, a plant that cannot run no longer bills a
  full crew, and a plant stopped for lack of cash plans its next run from real
  demand instead of losing its customers.
tags: [economy, corporations, balance]
badges: [patch]
areas: [engine]
---

- On the funded market a plant's run can now be paid out of the money that
  run earns the same turn. A company with little cash at the start of a turn
  can still run a plant whose sales cover its wages, site costs and
  materials. The amount it can borrow this way is capped at what the run is
  expected to earn, based on last turn's prices and how much of its output
  sold, with a margin for a weaker market.
- If the sales come in short, suppliers are still paid in full and the rest is
  owed to the workers and site payees as an ordinary bill, paid first out of
  the next turns' cash. No money is created.
- When a lack of cash cuts a plant's run, wages and running costs follow the
  part of the plant that actually ran. Idle capacity pays only its cold upkeep.
- A run cut by a lack of cash or materials is recorded as such, and the next
  turn plans from what the plant meant to make and how well that output sold,
  instead of treating the cut as weak demand and shrinking a step at a time.
