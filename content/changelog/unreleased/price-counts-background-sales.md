  including what the background economy sold, so goods whose buyers are
  being filled no longer read as short.

Until now the price of a good read supply without what the background
economy sold, while demand counted the orders those sales filled. A good could
look short to the price even when every buyer got what it ordered, and its
scarcity premium kept climbing toward the cap.

The price now counts those sales as supply, in the country (or state) where
they happened, the same total the market screens show.

What to expect:

- **Prices move no faster than before.** Each price still changes by at most
  2% a turn.
- **Fewer runaway premiums.** Goods mostly supplied by the background economy
  (natural gas, plastics, freight, fertilizers, advertising and software) stop
  climbing toward the price cap and settle near where they are.
- **Goods that are really short stay short.** Construction, healthcare,
  network and real estate services remain short after counting and keep their
  prices.
