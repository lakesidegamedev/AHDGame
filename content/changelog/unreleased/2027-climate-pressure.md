## What changed

- A 2027 world opens with climate pressure at 0.20 instead of zero, and each ton of emissions above a safe level now pushes it up faster. A world that cuts emissions quickly ends near 0.35 by 2050, one that follows current policies near 0.45, and one that keeps emitting at today's level near 0.6.
- Farms in hot and dry regions lose up to 15% of their harvest as pressure climbs toward 0.6. Precision agriculture halves the loss and cellular agriculture avoids it entirely.
- Households in hot regions spend more on power as summers get hotter: about 5% more at pressure 0.5.
- Farms and property companies in regions exposed to storms, floods, fire or drought pay rising insurance premiums, which eat into their margins.
- When a weather disaster strikes an exposed region, more of the damage lands on the national budget as pressure rises, because insurers pull back.
- Every country in the 2027 world now has hazard information: each region of the United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan and China, and a national profile for each of the other sixteen countries.
- Washington DC, Berlin, Saarland and the East and West Midlands had no hazard data and could never see a flood. They are now flood-prone in every world.
- Apart from those flood regions, worlds started from any other date, and the live world, behave exactly as before.
