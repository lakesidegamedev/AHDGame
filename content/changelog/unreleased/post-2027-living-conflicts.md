## What changed

- **AI labour shock (from 2029).** Countries where automation is widespread and unemployment is rising can be hit by a wave of AI layoffs. Leaders choose a retraining law, wage insurance, a robot tax or letting the market adjust. Doing nothing raises unemployment, cuts wage growth, costs approval and feeds an anti-automation backlash. The wave ends absorbed, reformed or entrenched.
- **Pacific strait crisis (2028 to 2045).** When world tension is high, a confrontation between China and the United States over a Pacific strait can begin. It climbs from posturing to exclusion zones, a shipping quarantine and an armed standoff, and every step has a way out through talks. Deterrence, talks, trade concessions and chip sanctions are the levers. Shipping disruption cuts electronics output, which pushes prices up. The crisis never starts a war on its own; a war still needs a formal declaration.
- **Infrastructure cyberattacks (from 2028).** Higher world tension and weaker power and network systems make attacks more likely. An intrusion can be contained before it causes outages. Outages hurt the grid, output and approval until repair crews restore service.
- A country is never caught in more than two serious ongoing crises at once. A third waits until one of them ends.
- Every crisis ends even if nobody acts, then rests for about two years before it can return.
